### Worked applications: models, calculations, and interpretation

These worked examples apply the module concepts. Each example states its conditions, explains the model, and shows the complete working.

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m8h1">Worked application 1: Helical motion and magnetic work</h4>

A charged particle enters a uniform, static magnetic field with nonzero velocity components parallel and perpendicular to the field. No other force acts.

The parallel and perpendicular velocity components explain the helical path and the constant speed.

1. **Separate the components.**

   $$v_\parallel=v\cos\theta,\quad v_\perp=v\sin\theta$$

2. **Check power.**

   $$P=q(\vec v\times\vec B)\cdot\vec v=0$$

3. **Write the radius.**

   $$r=mv_\perp/(|q|B)$$

4. **Combine the motions.** Uniform parallel motion and circular perpendicular motion produce a helix.

**Result:** The path is helical; speed and kinetic energy remain constant.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m8h2">Worked application 2: Compare radii at equal kinetic energy</h4>

Two nonrelativistic particles have the same charge magnitude and kinetic energy. Their masses are m and 2m. They enter the same uniform magnetic field perpendicularly.

The radius ratio compares the heavier particle with the lighter particle at equal kinetic energy and charge magnitude.

1. **Relate speed to energy.**

   $$K=\tfrac12mv^2\ \Rightarrow\ mv=\sqrt{2mK}$$

2. **Use the magnetic radius.**

   $$r=\frac{mv}{|q|B}=\frac{\sqrt{2mK}}{|q|B}$$

3. **Take the ratio.**

   $$r_{2m}/r_m=\sqrt2$$

4. **Check the conditions.** The result uses perpendicular, nonrelativistic motion and equal charge magnitudes.

**Result:** $\sqrt2$

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m8h3">Worked application 3: Changing only the charge sign</h4>

A particle’s charge changes from +q to −q. Mass, initial velocity, and the uniform magnetic field stay the same. The velocity is perpendicular to the field.

Reversing the charge sign reverses the magnetic-force direction while preserving the radius magnitude under the stated conditions.

1. **Reverse the force direction.**

   $$\vec F=(-q)(\vec v\times\vec B)$$

2. **Compare the radius.**

   $$r=mv/(|q|B)$$

3. **Compare the period.**

   $$T=2\pi m/(|q|B)$$

4. **Interpret the motion.** The particle bends in the opposite direction with the same speed and geometric radius.

**Result:** Curvature reverses; radius and period stay the same.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m8h4">Worked application 4: Loop plane versus loop normal</h4>

A current loop lies with its plane parallel to a uniform magnetic field. The loop current is nonzero.

The loop orientation determines the torque relative to its maximum possible magnitude.

1. **Identify the angle in the law.** Use the angle between the field and loop normal.

2. **Convert the stated plane orientation.**

   $$\theta_{\mathrm{normal}}=90^\circ$$

3. **Evaluate torque.**

   $$\tau=NIAB\sin90^\circ=NIAB$$

4. **Separate torque from net force.** A uniform field can give a loop torque while the net magnetic force on the closed loop is zero.

**Result:** It is maximum.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m8h5">Worked application 5: Parallel current directions</h4>

Two long parallel wires carry conventional currents in the same direction.

The wire field and magnetic force explain the interaction between parallel currents in the same direction.

1. **Find the field from one wire.**

   $$B=\mu_0 I/(2\pi d)$$

2. **Apply the right-hand rule.** At the other wire, use its current direction crossed with the local magnetic field.

3. **Find the force per length.**

   $$F/L=\mu_0 I_1I_2/(2\pi d)$$

4. **Check symmetry.** The forces point toward the opposite wires and have equal magnitudes per equal length.

**Result:** They attract each other.

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m8h6">Worked application 6: Radius of a proton helix</h4>

A proton of mass $1.673\times10^{-27}$ kg and charge $1.602\times10^{-19}$ C enters a uniform 0.010 T magnetic field at $1.00\times10^{5}$ m/s. Its velocity makes 30° with the field. The calculation determines the helix radius. Use nonrelativistic motion.

1. **Resolve perpendicular speed.**

   $$v_\perp=v\sin30^\circ=5.00\times10^4\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$$

2. **Balance perpendicular magnetic and centripetal forces.**

   $$|q|v_\perp B=mv_\perp^2/r$$

3. **Rearrange for the radius.**

   $$r=mv_\perp/(|q|B)$$

4. **Substitute SI values.**

   $$r=\frac{(1.673\times10^{-27})(5.00\times10^4)}{(1.602\times10^{-19})(0.010)}$$

5. **Check the other component.** The parallel component controls travel along the helix axis, not its radius.

6. **State the result.**

   $$0.052216\,\mathrm m$$

**Result:** $0.052216\,\mathrm m$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m8h7">Worked application 7: Torque when the angle is to the plane</h4>

A 20-turn rectangular loop measures 0.10 m by 0.20 m and carries 0.50 A in a 0.30 T uniform field. The field makes 30° with the loop plane. The calculation determines the torque magnitude.

1. **Calculate one-turn area.**

   $$A=(0.10)(0.20)=0.020\,\mathrm{m^2}$$

2. **Convert plane angle to normal angle.**

   $$\theta_n=90^\circ-30^\circ=60^\circ$$

3. **Use the normal-angle torque law.**

   $$\tau=NIAB\sin\theta_n$$

4. **Substitute.**

   $$\tau=(20)(0.50)(0.020)(0.30)\sin60^\circ$$

5. **Check the bound.** The result is below $NIAB=0.060\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, as required.

6. **State the result.**

   $$0.0519615\,\mathrm{N\,m}$$

**Result:** $0.0519615\,\mathrm{N\,m}$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m8h8">Worked application 8: Zero field between unequal wire currents</h4>

Two long straight wires lie parallel to the z axis at $x=0$ and $x=0.30\,\mathrm m$. They carry 2.0 A and 8.0 A in the same +z direction. The calculation determines the x coordinate between them where their net magnetic field vanishes.

1. **Determine the field directions.** Between same-direction wires, their fields point oppositely.

2. **Equate their magnitudes.**

   $$\frac{\mu_0(2)}{2\pi x}=\frac{\mu_0(8)}{2\pi(0.30-x)}$$

3. **Cancel constants and solve.**

   $$2(0.30-x)=8x\ \Rightarrow\ x=0.060\,\mathrm m$$

4. **Check the location.** The cancellation point is closer to the weaker-current wire.

5. **State the result.**

   $$0.06\,\mathrm m$$

**Result:** $0.06\,\mathrm m$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m8h9">Worked application 9: Signed magnetic-force component</h4>

A particle has charge −2.0 μC and velocity (300, 400, 0) m/s. The magnetic field is (0, 0, 0.50) T. The calculation determines the x component of its magnetic force in this right-handed coordinate system.

1. **Expand the cross product.**

   $$\vec v\times\vec B=(v_yB_z,-v_xB_z,0)=(200,-150,0)$$

2. **Multiply by the signed charge.**

   $$\vec F=(-2.0\times10^{-6})(200,-150,0)\,\mathrm N$$

3. **Read the requested component.**

   $$F_x=-4.00\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm N$$

4. **Check perpendicularity.**

   $$\vec F\cdot\vec v=(-0.0004)(300)+(0.0003)(400)=0$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$-4\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm N$$

**Result:** $-4\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm N$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m8h10">Worked application 10: Opposing coaxial solenoid fields</h4>

Two ideal long coaxial air-core solenoids overlap. Their uniform internal fields oppose. Solenoid 1 has 400 turns over 0.20 m and carries 1.5 A. Solenoid 2 has 200 turns over 0.20 m and carries 1.0 A. The calculation determines the net field magnitude in their common interior. Use $\mu_0=4\pi\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\,m\,A^{-1}}$.

1. **Calculate turn densities.**

   $$n_1=400/0.20=2000\,\mathrm{m^{-1}},\quad n_2=200/0.20=1000\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$$

2. **Choose the first field as positive.**

   $$B_{\mathrm{net}}=\mu_0(n_1I_1-n_2I_2)$$

3. **Substitute.**

   $$B_{\mathrm{net}}=(4\pi\times10^{-7})[2000(1.5)-1000(1.0)]$$

4. **Interpret the sign.** The positive result points along the first solenoid field. End effects and magnetic materials are excluded.

5. **State the result.**

   $$0.00251327\,\mathrm T$$

**Result:** $0.00251327\,\mathrm T$

</section>

<h4 id="solution-m8h11">Worked application 11: A velocity selector followed by a magnetic analyzer · the selected particle speed</h4>

A beam of protons passes straight through crossed electric and magnetic fields. Their directions make the electric and magnetic forces oppose. The selector has $E=3000\,\mathrm{N\,C^{-1}}$ and $B_1=0.02\,\mathrm T$. The beam then enters a separate perpendicular uniform field $B_2=0.1\,\mathrm T$. Use proton mass $1.673\times10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and charge $+1.602\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$. Ignore all other forces and relativistic effects. The calculation determines the selected particle speed.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Straight selector motion requires electric and magnetic forces to cancel. The analyzer changes direction without changing speed.

2. **Balance selector force magnitudes.**

   $$qE=qvB_1\ \Rightarrow\ v=E/B_1=150000\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$$

3. **Balance analyzer force with centripetal acceleration.**

   $$qvB_2=mv^2/r\ \Rightarrow\ r=mv/(qB_2)$$

4. **Relate kinetic energy and period to the selected speed.**

   $$K=\tfrac12mv^2,\quad T=2\pi r/v=2\pi m/(qB_2)$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$v=3000/0.02=150000\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$$

6. **Check the physical result.** The test-particle charge magnitude cancels from the selector speed.

7. **State the result.**

   $$150000\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$$

**Result:** $150000\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$

</section>

<h4 id="solution-m8h21">Worked application 21: A wire supported by magnetic force · the upward magnetic-force magnitude</h4>

A horizontal straight wire segment has length 0.3 m, mass 0.02 kg, and conventional current 3 A. A horizontal uniform magnetic field of 0.5 T makes 40° with the wire. The current and field directions give upward magnetic force. Only gravity and magnetic force act vertically after support is removed. Use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. The calculation determines the upward magnetic-force magnitude.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Use the angle between current direction and magnetic field. Subtract weight when calculating the net vertical acceleration.

2. **Calculate upward magnetic force.**

   $$F_B=ILB\sin\theta=0.289254\,\mathrm N$$

3. **Keep gravity in the net-force balance.**

   $$ma_y=F_B-mg$$

4. **State the levitation condition.**

   $$I_{\rm bal}LB\sin\theta=mg$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$F_B=(3)(0.3)(0.5)\sin40^\circ$$

6. **Check the physical result.** The magnetic force is below ILB because the angle is less than 90 degrees.

7. **State the result.**

   $$0.289254\,\mathrm N$$

**Result:** $0.289254\,\mathrm N$

</section>

<h4 id="solution-m8h23">Worked application 23: A wire supported by magnetic force · Keeping the field unchanged, the current magnitude needed for levitation</h4>

A horizontal straight wire segment has length 0.3 m, mass 0.02 kg, and conventional current 3 A. A horizontal uniform magnetic field of 0.5 T makes 40° with the wire. The current and field directions give upward magnetic force. Only gravity and magnetic force act vertically after support is removed. Use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. Keeping the field unchanged, the calculation determines the current magnitude needed for levitation.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Use the angle between current direction and magnetic field. Subtract weight when calculating the net vertical acceleration.

2. **Calculate upward magnetic force.**

   $$F_B=ILB\sin\theta=0.289254\,\mathrm N$$

3. **Keep gravity in the net-force balance.**

   $$ma_y=F_B-mg$$

4. **State the levitation condition.**

   $$I_{\rm bal}LB\sin\theta=mg$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$I_{\rm bal}=mg/(LB\sin\theta)=2.03489\,\mathrm A$$

<h4 id="solution-m8h51">Worked application 51: Infer velocity from a measured helix · the perpendicular speed</h4>

A proton follows a helix in a uniform magnetic field of 0.01 T. The helix radius is 0.025 m and the distance along the field per complete turn is 0.08 m. Use proton mass $1.673\times10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and charge $+1.602\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$. Assume nonrelativistic motion and no other force. The calculation determines the perpendicular speed.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Radius determines perpendicular speed. Pitch divided by the cyclotron period determines parallel speed. Both components contribute to kinetic energy.

2. **Find the period from charge, mass, and field.**

   $$T=2\pi m/(qB)=6.56165\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm s$$

3. **Recover both velocity components.**

   $$v_\perp=qBr/m=23939\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}},\quad v_\parallel=p/T=12192\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$$

4. **Combine the orthogonal speeds.**

   $$v^2=v_\perp^2+v_\parallel^2,\quad\tan\theta=v_\perp/v_\parallel$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$v_\perp=qBr/m=23939\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$$

6. **Check the physical result.** Only the velocity component perpendicular to the field bends into a circle.

7. **State the result.**

   $$23939\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$$

**Result:** $23939\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$
