### Situational problems: choose the law, then solve

These added practice problems apply the module concepts. They are not copied examination questions. Keep the stated assumptions when you use each result.

#### Situation 1: Helical motion and magnetic work

A charged particle enters a uniform, static magnetic field with nonzero velocity components parallel and perpendicular to the field. No other force acts. Which statement is correct?

1. **Separate the components.**

   $$v_\parallel=v\cos\theta,\quad v_\perp=v\sin\theta$$

2. **Check power.**

   $$P=q(\vec v\times\vec B)\cdot\vec v=0$$

3. **Write the radius.**

   $$r=mv_\perp/(|q|B)$$

4. **Combine the motions.** Uniform parallel motion and circular perpendicular motion produce a helix.

**Answer:** The path is helical; speed and kinetic energy remain constant.

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **The field steadily increases kinetic energy.** — The magnetic force is perpendicular to velocity.
- **The parallel velocity decreases until the motion is circular.** — The magnetic force has no component along the field.
- **The force always points along the magnetic field.** — The cross product gives a force perpendicular to the field.

#### Situation 2: Compare radii at equal kinetic energy

Two nonrelativistic particles have the same charge magnitude and kinetic energy. Their masses are m and 2m. They enter the same uniform magnetic field perpendicularly. What is the heavier particle’s radius divided by the lighter particle’s radius?

1. **Relate speed to energy.**

   $$K=\tfrac12mv^2\ \Rightarrow\ mv=\sqrt{2mK}$$

2. **Use the magnetic radius.**

   $$r=\frac{mv}{|q|B}=\frac{\sqrt{2mK}}{|q|B}$$

3. **Take the ratio.**

   $$r_{2m}/r_m=\sqrt2$$

4. **Check the conditions.** The result uses perpendicular, nonrelativistic motion and equal charge magnitudes.

**Answer:** $\sqrt2$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$2$** — This would apply at equal speed, not equal kinetic energy.
- **$1/\sqrt2$** — The heavier particle has larger momentum at the same kinetic energy.
- **$1$** — Equal kinetic energy does not imply equal momentum.

#### Situation 3: Changing only the charge sign

A particle’s charge changes from +q to −q. Mass, initial velocity, and the uniform magnetic field stay the same. The velocity is perpendicular to the field. What changes?

1. **Reverse the force direction.**

   $$\vec F=(-q)(\vec v\times\vec B)$$

2. **Compare the radius.**

   $$r=mv/(|q|B)$$

3. **Compare the period.**

   $$T=2\pi m/(|q|B)$$

4. **Interpret the motion.** The particle bends in the opposite direction with the same speed and geometric radius.

**Answer:** Curvature reverses; radius and period stay the same.

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **Radius becomes negative as a physical length.** — The radius is a nonnegative length. Orientation can use a signed convention separately.
- **The period doubles.** — The charge magnitude, field, and mass stay the same.
- **Speed increases because the force reverses.** — Magnetic force still does no work.

#### Situation 4: Loop plane versus loop normal

A current loop lies with its plane parallel to a uniform magnetic field. The loop current is nonzero. What is the torque magnitude relative to its maximum possible value?

1. **Identify the angle in the law.** Use the angle between the field and loop normal.

2. **Convert the stated plane orientation.**

   $$\theta_{\mathrm{normal}}=90^\circ$$

3. **Evaluate torque.**

   $$\tau=NIAB\sin90^\circ=NIAB$$

4. **Separate torque from net force.** A uniform field can give a loop torque while the net magnetic force on the closed loop is zero.

**Answer:** It is maximum.

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **It is zero.** — Zero torque occurs when the loop normal is parallel or antiparallel to the field.
- **It is half the maximum.** — The given geometry corresponds to 90° between the normal and field.
- **It cannot be determined without the loop perimeter.** — The relative torque follows from the angle alone.

#### Situation 5: Parallel current directions

Two long parallel wires carry conventional currents in the same direction. Which statement describes their magnetic interaction?

1. **Find the field from one wire.**

   $$B=\mu_0 I/(2\pi d)$$

2. **Apply the right-hand rule.** At the other wire, use its current direction crossed with the local magnetic field.

3. **Find the force per length.**

   $$F/L=\mu_0 I_1I_2/(2\pi d)$$

4. **Check symmetry.** The forces point toward the opposite wires and have equal magnitudes per equal length.

**Answer:** They attract each other.

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **They repel because the electrons move opposite conventional current.** — The standard wire-force law already uses conventional current consistently.
- **Only the larger-current wire feels a force.** — Both wires exert equal-magnitude, opposite forces per equal length.
- **They have zero force because their currents are parallel.** — The force uses the field made by the other wire, not that wire’s current direction as the field direction.

#### Situation 6: Radius of a proton helix

A proton of mass $1.673\times10^{-27}$ kg and charge $1.602\times10^{-19}$ C enters a uniform 0.010 T magnetic field at $1.00\times10^{5}$ m/s. Its velocity makes 30° with the field. Find the helix radius. Use nonrelativistic motion.

1. **Resolve perpendicular speed.**

   $$v_\perp=v\sin30^\circ=5.00\times10^4\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$$

2. **Balance perpendicular magnetic and centripetal forces.**

   $$|q|v_\perp B=mv_\perp^2/r$$

3. **Rearrange for the radius.**

   $$r=mv_\perp/(|q|B)$$

4. **Substitute SI values.**

   $$r=\frac{(1.673\times10^{-27})(5.00\times10^4)}{(1.602\times10^{-19})(0.010)}$$

5. **Check the other component.** The parallel component controls travel along the helix axis, not its radius.

6. **State the result.**

   $$0.052216\,\mathrm m$$

**Answer:** $0.052216\,\mathrm m$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$0.104432\,\mathrm m$** — This uses total speed instead of perpendicular speed.
- **$0.0904407\,\mathrm m$** — This uses the parallel component.
- **$0.026108\,\mathrm m$** — This doubles the magnetic field without justification.

#### Situation 7: Torque when the angle is to the plane

A 20-turn rectangular loop measures 0.10 m by 0.20 m and carries 0.50 A in a 0.30 T uniform field. The field makes 30° with the loop plane. Find the torque magnitude.

1. **Calculate one-turn area.**

   $$A=(0.10)(0.20)=0.020\,\mathrm{m^2}$$

2. **Convert plane angle to normal angle.**

   $$\theta_n=90^\circ-30^\circ=60^\circ$$

3. **Use the normal-angle torque law.**

   $$\tau=NIAB\sin\theta_n$$

4. **Substitute.**

   $$\tau=(20)(0.50)(0.020)(0.30)\sin60^\circ$$

5. **Check the bound.** The result is below $NIAB=0.060\,\mathrm{N\,m}$, as required.

6. **State the result.**

   $$0.0519615\,\mathrm{N\,m}$$

**Answer:** $0.0519615\,\mathrm{N\,m}$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$0.03\,\mathrm{N\,m}$** — This puts the plane angle directly into the sine.
- **$0.06\,\mathrm{N\,m}$** — This assumes maximum torque.
- **$0.003\,\mathrm{N\,m}$** — This omits the number of turns while also using the maximum-angle value.

#### Situation 8: Zero field between unequal wire currents

Two long straight wires lie parallel to the z axis at $x=0$ and $x=0.30\,\mathrm m$. They carry 2.0 A and 8.0 A in the same +z direction. Find the x coordinate between them where their net magnetic field vanishes.

1. **Determine the field directions.** Between same-direction wires, their fields point oppositely.

2. **Equate their magnitudes.**

   $$\frac{\mu_0(2)}{2\pi x}=\frac{\mu_0(8)}{2\pi(0.30-x)}$$

3. **Cancel constants and solve.**

   $$2(0.30-x)=8x\ \Rightarrow\ x=0.060\,\mathrm m$$

4. **Check the location.** The cancellation point is closer to the weaker-current wire.

5. **State the result.**

   $$0.06\,\mathrm m$$

**Answer:** $0.06\,\mathrm m$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$0.1\,\mathrm m$** — This uses an inverse-square field instead of the straight-wire inverse-distance law.
- **$0.15\,\mathrm m$** — The midpoint cancels only for equal currents.
- **$0.24\,\mathrm m$** — This measures from the stronger-current wire while the question measures from $x=0$.

#### Situation 9: Signed magnetic-force component

A particle has charge −2.0 μC and velocity (300, 400, 0) m/s. The magnetic field is (0, 0, 0.50) T. Find the x component of its magnetic force in this right-handed coordinate system.

1. **Expand the cross product.**

   $$\vec v\times\vec B=(v_yB_z,-v_xB_z,0)=(200,-150,0)$$

2. **Multiply by the signed charge.**

   $$\vec F=(-2.0\times10^{-6})(200,-150,0)\,\mathrm N$$

3. **Read the requested component.**

   $$F_x=-4.00\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm N$$

4. **Check perpendicularity.**

   $$\vec F\cdot\vec v=(-0.0004)(300)+(0.0003)(400)=0$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$-4\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm N$$

**Answer:** $-4\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm N$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$4\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm N$** — This misses the negative charge sign.
- **$-3\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm N$** — This uses the x velocity in the x force component.
- **$5\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm N$** — This is the total force magnitude, not its signed x component.

#### Situation 10: Opposing coaxial solenoid fields

Two ideal long coaxial air-core solenoids overlap. Their uniform internal fields oppose. Solenoid 1 has 400 turns over 0.20 m and carries 1.5 A. Solenoid 2 has 200 turns over 0.20 m and carries 1.0 A. Find the net field magnitude in their common interior. Use $\mu_0=4\pi\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{T\,m\,A^{-1}}$.

1. **Calculate turn densities.**

   $$n_1=400/0.20=2000\,\mathrm{m^{-1}},\quad n_2=200/0.20=1000\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$$

2. **Choose the first field as positive.**

   $$B_{\mathrm{net}}=\mu_0(n_1I_1-n_2I_2)$$

3. **Substitute.**

   $$B_{\mathrm{net}}=(4\pi\times10^{-7})[2000(1.5)-1000(1.0)]$$

4. **Interpret the sign.** The positive result points along the first solenoid field. End effects and magnetic materials are excluded.

5. **State the result.**

   $$0.00251327\,\mathrm T$$

**Answer:** $0.00251327\,\mathrm T$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$0.00502655\,\mathrm T$** — This adds fields that the question states oppose.
- **$0.00125664\,\mathrm T$** — This gives the second solenoid field alone.
- **$0.00376991\,\mathrm T$** — This gives the first solenoid field alone.

<h4 id="solution-m8h11">Situation 11: A velocity selector followed by a magnetic analyzer · Find the selected particle speed</h4>

A beam of protons passes straight through crossed electric and magnetic fields. Their directions make the electric and magnetic forces oppose. The selector has $E=3000\,\mathrm{N\,C^{-1}}$ and $B_1=0.02\,\mathrm T$. The beam then enters a separate perpendicular uniform field $B_2=0.1\,\mathrm T$. Use proton mass $1.673\times10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and charge $+1.602\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$. Ignore all other forces and relativistic effects. Find the selected particle speed.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Straight selector motion requires electric and magnetic forces to cancel. The analyzer changes direction without changing speed.

2. **Balance selector force magnitudes.**

   $$qE=qvB_1\ \Rightarrow\ v=E/B_1=150000\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$$

3. **Balance analyzer force with centripetal acceleration.**

   $$qvB_2=mv^2/r\ \Rightarrow\ r=mv/(qB_2)$$

4. **Relate kinetic energy and period to the selected speed.**

   $$K=\tfrac12mv^2,\quad T=2\pi r/v=2\pi m/(qB_2)$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$v=3000/0.02=150000\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$$

6. **Check the physical result.** The test-particle charge magnitude cancels from the selector speed.

7. **State the result.**

   $$150000\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$$

**Answer:** $150000\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$30000\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$** — This uses the analyzer field instead of the selector field.
- **$60\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$** — The force balance requires division by B, not multiplication.
- **$300000\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$** — Both forces act on one particle; their balance does not double the speed.

<h4 id="solution-m8h15">Situation 15: A velocity selector followed by a magnetic analyzer · model check</h4>

   $$K=\tfrac12mv^2,\quad T=2\pi r/v=2\pi m/(qB_2)$$

6. **Check the physical result.** Only the velocity component perpendicular to the field bends into a circle.

7. **State the result.**

   $$23939\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$$

**Answer:** $23939\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$12192\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$** — Pitch determines the parallel component, not the perpendicular component.
