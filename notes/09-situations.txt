### Worked applications: models, calculations, and interpretation

These worked examples apply the module concepts. Each example states its conditions, explains the model, and shows the complete working.

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m9h1">Worked application 1: Zero flux does not mean zero emf</h4>

A rigid loop rotates at constant angular speed in a uniform magnetic field. At one instant its magnetic flux is zero.

The rotating-loop model compares instantaneous flux with induced emf when the flux is zero.

1. **Write the flux.**

   $$\Phi=BA\cos(\omega t)$$

2. **Differentiate it.**

   $$\mathcal E=-N\frac{d\Phi}{dt}=NBA\omega\sin(\omega t)$$

3. **Check the zero-flux orientation.**

   $$\cos(\omega t)=0\ \Rightarrow\ |\sin(\omega t)|=1$$

4. **Keep the distinction.** The field, area, or orientation can change the flux.

**Result:** Yes. For sinusoidal flux, its magnitude is maximum when flux crosses zero.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m9h2">Worked application 2: Lenz direction for a weakening field</h4>

A conducting loop lies in the page. A uniform external magnetic field points out of the page and decreases in magnitude.

Lenz’s law determines the induced conventional-current direction, viewed from the front, as the outward field decreases.

1. **Identify the change.** Outward flux is decreasing.

2. **Oppose that change.** The induced field must point outward.

3. **Apply the loop right-hand rule.** An outward thumb gives counterclockwise curled fingers.

4. **Check the wording.** The induced field opposes the change, rather than always opposing the external field.

**Result:** Counterclockwise.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m9h3">Worked application 3: The same initial and final flux</h4>

The magnetic flux through a fixed N-turn coil changes and later returns to its initial value.

Faraday’s law determines the time integral of the induced emf over the entire interval when the initial flux returns.

1. **Start from Faraday’s law.**

   $$\mathcal E=-N\,d\Phi/dt$$

2. **Integrate with a fixed orientation.**

   $$\int_{t_i}^{t_f}\mathcal E\,dt=-N(\Phi_f-\Phi_i)$$

3. **Use the equal endpoint values.**

   $$\Phi_f=\Phi_i\ \Rightarrow\ \int\mathcal E\,dt=0$$

4. **Avoid an extra conclusion.** The emf can be nonzero during parts of the interval.

**Result:** It is zero: $\int\mathcal E\,dt=0$.

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m9h4">Worked application 4: A transformer after the DC transient</h4>

A transformer primary has nonzero winding resistance. A DC source has been connected long enough for its current and core flux to become constant. Ignore leakage and saturation.

The constant final core flux determines the secondary induced emf after the DC transient ends.

1. **Identify the stated steady condition.** After the transient, the current and flux are constant.

2. **Apply Faraday’s law to the secondary.**

   $$\mathcal E_s=-N_s\,d\Phi/dt$$

3. **Use the zero derivative.**

   $$d\Phi/dt=0\ \Rightarrow\ \mathcal E_s=0$$

4. **Separate switching from steady operation.** Connection and disconnection can produce transient secondary emf.

**Result:** Zero.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m9h5">Worked application 5: An inductor starts to discharge</h4>

An inductor carrying current is disconnected from its source and connected into a closed loop with a finite resistor.

Inductor-current continuity determines the current immediately after switching into the closed resistor loop.

1. **Use the inductor relation.**

   $$V_L=L\,di/dt$$

2. **Write the source-free loop equation.**

   $$L\,di/dt+Ri=0$$

3. **Use current continuity.**

   $$i(t)=I_0e^{-Rt/L},\quad i(0^+)=I_0$$

4. **Check energy transfer.** The resistor dissipates the magnetic energy initially stored in the inductor.

**Result:** Current starts from its previous value and then decays continuously.

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m9h6">Worked application 6: Both field and area change</h4>

A 100-turn coil has a uniform perpendicular field $B(t)=(0.20+0.10t)\,\mathrm T$ and area $A(t)=(0.030-0.002t)\,\mathrm{m^2}$, with t in seconds. Choose positive flux along the field and positive emf around its right-hand-rule contour. The calculation determines the signed induced emf at $t=2.0\,\mathrm s$.

1. **Use flux per turn.**

   $$\Phi(t)=B(t)A(t)$$

2. **Differentiate both changing factors.**

   $$\mathcal E=-N[A\,dB/dt+B\,dA/dt]$$

3. **Evaluate the instantaneous quantities.**

   $$B(2)=0.40\,\mathrm T,\quad A(2)=0.026\,\mathrm{m^2}$$

4. **Substitute both contributions.**

   $$\mathcal E=-100[(0.026)(0.10)+(0.40)(-0.002)]=-0.180\,\mathrm V$$

5. **Interpret the sign.** Field growth dominates area shrinkage, so positive flux increases and the emf acts in the negative contour direction.

6. **State the result.**

   $$-0.18\,\mathrm V$$

**Result:** $-0.18\,\mathrm V$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m9h7">Worked application 7: Emf of a rotating coil</h4>

A 100-turn coil of area 0.010 m² rotates in a uniform 0.20 T field at 50 rad/s. Its normal makes an increasing angle of 30° with the field. The calculation determines the instantaneous induced emf magnitude.

1. **Write the angle-dependent flux.**

   $$\Phi=BA\cos\theta$$

2. **Differentiate using angular speed.**

   $$|\mathcal E|=NBA\omega|\sin\theta|$$

3. **Substitute.**

   $$|\mathcal E|=(100)(0.20)(0.010)(50)\sin30^\circ=5.00\,\mathrm V$$

4. **Check the angle.** Use the angle to the normal. The maximum occurs at a normal angle of 90°.

5. **State the result.**

   $$5\,\mathrm V$$

**Result:** $5\,\mathrm V$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m9h8">Worked application 8: Mechanical power in a sliding rod</h4>

A 0.40 m conducting rod slides at constant 3.0 m/s on ideal rails in a uniform 0.50 T field. The rod, velocity, and field are mutually perpendicular. The closed circuit has total resistance 2.0 Ω. Ignore friction and self-inductance. The calculation determines the external mechanical power needed.

1. **Find motional emf.**

   $$\mathcal E=BLv=(0.50)(0.40)(3.0)=0.60\,\mathrm V$$

2. **Find the current.**

   $$I=\mathcal E/R=0.60/2.0=0.30\,\mathrm A$$

3. **Find magnetic drag and balance it.**

   $$F_{\mathrm{ext}}=BIL=(0.50)(0.30)(0.40)=0.060\,\mathrm N$$

4. **Calculate mechanical power.**

   $$P=F_{\mathrm{ext}}v=(0.060)(3.0)=0.180\,\mathrm W$$

5. **Check electrical heating.**

   $$I^2R=(0.30)^2(2.0)=0.180\,\mathrm W$$

6. **State the result.**

   $$0.18\,\mathrm W$$

**Result:** $0.18\,\mathrm W$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m9h9">Worked application 9: An RL circuit reaches a chosen current</h4>

A 12 V ideal source is connected to a series 6.0 Ω resistor and 3.0 H ideal inductor. The initial current is zero. The calculation determines the time when current first reaches 75% of its final value.

1. **Calculate the final current and time constant.**

   $$I_\infty=12/6=2.0\,\mathrm A,\quad\tau=L/R=3/6=0.50\,\mathrm s$$

2. **Use the growth solution.**

   $$i/I_\infty=1-e^{-t/\tau}$$

3. **Isolate the remaining fraction.**

   $$e^{-t/\tau}=1-0.75=0.25$$

4. **Take logarithms.**

   $$t=-0.50\ln0.25=0.50\ln4$$

5. **Check the time.** Reaching 75% takes more than one time constant.

6. **State the result.**

   $$0.693147\,\mathrm s$$

**Result:** $0.693147\,\mathrm s$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m9h10">Worked application 10: Transformer input current under load</h4>

An ideal transformer receives 24 V rms at its 100-turn primary. Its 500-turn secondary supplies a 240 Ω resistor. Use sinusoidal steady operation. The calculation determines the primary rms current.

1. **Find the secondary rms voltage.**

   $$V_s=V_pN_s/N_p=24(500/100)=120\,\mathrm V$$

2. **Find secondary current.**

   $$I_s=V_s/R=120/240=0.50\,\mathrm A$$

3. **Use ideal power conservation.**

   $$V_pI_p=V_sI_s$$

4. **Solve the primary current.**

   $$I_p=(120)(0.50)/24=2.50\,\mathrm A$$

5. **Check power.**

   $$P_{\mathrm{in}}=24(2.50)=60\,\mathrm W=P_{\mathrm{load}}$$

6. **State the result.**

   $$2.5\,\mathrm A$$

**Result:** $2.5\,\mathrm A$

</section>

<h4 id="solution-m9h11">Worked application 11: An RL circuit with initial current · the current at the stated time</h4>

A series RL loop has resistance 6 Ω, inductance 3 H, and ideal source 12 V. Just before a switch establishes this loop, the inductor current is 0.3 A in the source-driven positive direction. The switch preserves a closed finite-resistance path. The calculation determines values at 0.3 s. Define inductor voltage as the passive drop along positive current. The calculation determines the current at the stated time.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Inductor current is continuous. Use the general exponential solution with the actual initial current, whether it is above or below the new steady value.

2. **Find the steady current and time constant.**

   $$I_\infty=V_s/R=2\,\mathrm A,\quad\tau=L/R=0.5\,\mathrm s$$

3. **Use the arbitrary-initial-current solution.**

   $$I(t)=I_\infty+(I_0-I_\infty)e^{-t/\tau}=1.06702\,\mathrm A$$

4. **Use the passive inductor-voltage convention.**

   $$V_L=L\,dI/dt=V_s-RI=5.59788\,\mathrm V$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$I=2+(0.3-2)e^{-0.3/0.5}$$

6. **Check the physical result.** The current lies between its initial value and the source-defined final value.

7. **State the result.**

   $$1.06702\,\mathrm A$$

**Result:** $1.06702\,\mathrm A$

</section>

<h4 id="solution-m9h21">Worked application 21: Where the inductor energy goes · the current at the stated time</h4>

An inductor of 1.8 H initially carries 1.5 A. It discharges into a closed 9 Ω resistor loop with no source. Neglect other losses. Consider time 0.08 s after the decay begins. The current reference follows the initial current. The calculation determines the current at the stated time.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Current decays with time constant L/R. Energy depends on current squared, so it decays twice as fast in the exponential.

2. **Find the current decay.**

   $$I=I_0e^{-Rt/L}=1.00548\,\mathrm A$$

3. **Find the remaining and dissipated energies.**

   $$U_L=\tfrac12LI_0^2e^{-2Rt/L},\quad Q_R=\tfrac12LI_0^2-U_L$$

4. **Derive the energy half-time.**

   $$e^{-2Rt_{1/2}/L}=1/2\ \Rightarrow\ t_{1/2}=\frac{L\ln2}{2R}$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$I=1.5e^{-9(0.08)/1.8}$$

6. **Check the physical result.** The current remains in its original direction while its magnitude decays.

7. **State the result.**

   $$1.00548\,\mathrm A$$

**Result:** $1.00548\,\mathrm A$

</section>

A conducting rod of length 0.4 m slides at constant speed 4 m/s on ideal rails in a uniform perpendicular field of 0.50 T. Rod, velocity, and field are mutually perpendicular. Its motional emf drives the positive loop direction. A 0.3 V ideal battery opposes that direction. Total circuit resistance is 2.0 Ω. Ignore friction and self-inductance. Define external force positive along the rod velocity; define battery power positive when absorbed. The calculation determines the signed power absorbed by the battery.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Add the signed battery and motional emfs. Current reversal can turn magnetic drag into a magnetic driving force.

2. **Calculate motional emf.**

   $$\mathcal E_m=BLv=0.8\,\mathrm V$$

3. **Apply the signed loop equation.**

   $$I=(\mathcal E_m-\mathcal E_b)/R=0.25\,\mathrm A$$

4. **Balance the magnetic force at constant speed.**

   $$F_{\rm ext}=BLI,\quad P_{\rm ext}=F_{\rm ext}v$$

5. **Check the power balance.**

   $$\mathcal E_mI=I^2R+\mathcal E_bI$$

6. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$P_b=\mathcal E_bI=0.3(0.25)$$

7. **Check the physical result.** A negative result means the battery supplies electrical power.

8. **State the result.**

   $$0.075\,\mathrm W$$

**Result:** $0.075\,\mathrm W$

<h4 id="solution-m9h51">Worked application 51: A transformer with stated efficiency · the secondary rms voltage</h4>

A transformer operates on sinusoidal AC with primary rms voltage 24 V. It has 100 primary turns and 500 secondary turns. A 240 Ω resistor loads the secondary. Use a model with the ideal turns voltage ratio, unity primary power factor, and total efficiency 0.9. All losses are represented by that efficiency. The calculation determines the secondary rms voltage.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Use the turns ratio for voltage under the stated model. Use efficiency for real input power before converting that power to primary rms current.

2. **Find secondary voltage and current.**

   $$V_s=V_pN_s/N_p=120\,\mathrm V,\quad I_s=V_s/R=0.5\,\mathrm A$$

3. **Include efficiency in the power balance.**

   $$P_{\rm out}=V_sI_s=60\,\mathrm W,\quad P_{\rm in}=P_{\rm out}/\eta=66.6667\,\mathrm W$$

4. **Use the specified unity primary power factor.**

   $$I_p=P_{\rm in}/V_p$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$V_s=24(500/100)=120\,\mathrm V$$

6. **Check the physical result.** The voltage follows the explicitly stated turns-ratio model, not an extra efficiency factor.

7. **State the result.**

   $$120\,\mathrm V$$

**Result:** $120\,\mathrm V$
