### Situational problems: choose the law, then solve

These added practice problems apply the module concepts. They are not copied examination questions. Keep the stated assumptions when you use each result.

#### Situation 1: Zero flux does not mean zero emf

A rigid loop rotates at constant angular speed in a uniform magnetic field. At one instant its magnetic flux is zero. Can the induced emf magnitude be nonzero at that instant?

1. **Write the flux.**

   $$\Phi=BA\cos(\omega t)$$

2. **Differentiate it.**

   $$\mathcal E=-N\frac{d\Phi}{dt}=NBA\omega\sin(\omega t)$$

3. **Check the zero-flux orientation.**

   $$\cos(\omega t)=0\ \Rightarrow\ |\sin(\omega t)|=1$$

4. **Keep the distinction.** The field, area, or orientation can change the flux.

**Answer:** Yes. For sinusoidal flux, its magnitude is maximum when flux crosses zero.

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **No. Zero flux always gives zero emf.** — This confuses a value with its derivative.
- **Only if the magnetic field magnitude increases.** — Changing loop orientation can change flux even when the field magnitude is constant.
- **No. A rigid loop cannot have changing flux.** — The loop area can stay fixed while its angle changes.

#### Situation 2: Lenz direction for a weakening field

A conducting loop lies in the page. A uniform external magnetic field points out of the page and decreases in magnitude. Viewed from the front, which way does the induced conventional current flow?

1. **Identify the change.** Outward flux is decreasing.

2. **Oppose that change.** The induced field must point outward.

3. **Apply the loop right-hand rule.** An outward thumb gives counterclockwise curled fingers.

4. **Check the wording.** The induced field opposes the change, rather than always opposing the external field.

**Answer:** Counterclockwise.

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **Clockwise.** — That produces an inward field and reinforces the loss of outward flux.
- **There is no current because the field does not reverse.** — A changing magnitude is enough to induce emf.
- **The direction cannot be found without the resistance.** — Positive resistance affects magnitude, not the Lenz-law direction.

#### Situation 3: The same initial and final flux

The magnetic flux through a fixed N-turn coil changes and later returns to its initial value. What follows for the time integral of its induced emf over the entire interval?

1. **Start from Faraday’s law.**

   $$\mathcal E=-N\,d\Phi/dt$$

2. **Integrate with a fixed orientation.**

   $$\int_{t_i}^{t_f}\mathcal E\,dt=-N(\Phi_f-\Phi_i)$$

3. **Use the equal endpoint values.**

   $$\Phi_f=\Phi_i\ \Rightarrow\ \int\mathcal E\,dt=0$$

4. **Avoid an extra conclusion.** The emf can be nonzero during parts of the interval.

**Answer:** It is zero: $\int\mathcal E\,dt=0$.

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **The emf is zero at every instant.** — A zero integral does not require a zero integrand at every instant.
- **The integral must be positive.** — Positive and negative contributions can cancel.
- **The coil cannot exchange energy during the interval.** — A zero emf integral alone does not determine electrical energy transfer.

#### Situation 4: A transformer after the DC transient

A transformer primary has nonzero winding resistance. A DC source has been connected long enough for its current and core flux to become constant. Ignore leakage and saturation. What is the secondary induced emf now?

1. **Identify the stated steady condition.** After the transient, the current and flux are constant.

2. **Apply Faraday’s law to the secondary.**

   $$\mathcal E_s=-N_s\,d\Phi/dt$$

3. **Use the zero derivative.**

   $$d\Phi/dt=0\ \Rightarrow\ \mathcal E_s=0$$

4. **Separate switching from steady operation.** Connection and disconnection can produce transient secondary emf.

**Answer:** Zero.

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **It follows the ordinary AC turns ratio with the DC source voltage.** — The AC turns-ratio calculation requires changing linked flux.
- **It increases continuously with time.** — The question states the flux is constant.
- **It is nonzero because the core flux itself is nonzero.** — Flux magnitude alone does not determine induced emf.

#### Situation 5: An inductor starts to discharge

An inductor carrying current is disconnected from its source and connected into a closed loop with a finite resistor. Which statement is correct immediately after switching in the ideal RL model?

1. **Use the inductor relation.**

   $$V_L=L\,di/dt$$

2. **Write the source-free loop equation.**

   $$L\,di/dt+Ri=0$$

3. **Use current continuity.**

   $$i(t)=I_0e^{-Rt/L},\quad i(0^+)=I_0$$

4. **Check energy transfer.** The resistor dissipates the magnetic energy initially stored in the inductor.

**Answer:** Current starts from its previous value and then decays continuously.

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **Current instantly becomes zero.** — That would require an impulse of voltage across the ideal inductor.
- **Current reverses instantly to oppose the old current.** — Lenz’s law opposes the decrease of current, not the preexisting current itself.
- **The inductor creates energy to keep the current constant forever.** — The initial magnetic energy becomes resistor heat.

#### Situation 6: Both field and area change

A 100-turn coil has a uniform perpendicular field $B(t)=(0.20+0.10t)\,\mathrm T$ and area $A(t)=(0.030-0.002t)\,\mathrm{m^2}$, with t in seconds. Choose positive flux along the field and positive emf around its right-hand-rule contour. Find the signed induced emf at $t=2.0\,\mathrm s$.

1. **Use flux per turn.**

   $$\Phi(t)=B(t)A(t)$$

2. **Differentiate both changing factors.**

   $$\mathcal E=-N[A\,dB/dt+B\,dA/dt]$$

3. **Evaluate the instantaneous quantities.**

   $$B(2)=0.40\,\mathrm T,\quad A(2)=0.026\,\mathrm{m^2}$$

4. **Substitute both contributions.**

   $$\mathcal E=-100[(0.026)(0.10)+(0.40)(-0.002)]=-0.180\,\mathrm V$$

5. **Interpret the sign.** Field growth dominates area shrinkage, so positive flux increases and the emf acts in the negative contour direction.

6. **State the result.**

   $$-0.18\,\mathrm V$$

**Answer:** $-0.18\,\mathrm V$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$-0.26\,\mathrm V$** — This differentiates only the field and holds the changing area fixed.
- **$0.08\,\mathrm V$** — This uses only the area-change contribution and its sign.
- **$0.18\,\mathrm V$** — This misses Faraday’s minus sign for the chosen contour.

#### Situation 7: Emf of a rotating coil

A 100-turn coil of area 0.010 m² rotates in a uniform 0.20 T field at 50 rad/s. Its normal makes an increasing angle of 30° with the field. Find the instantaneous induced emf magnitude.

1. **Write the angle-dependent flux.**

   $$\Phi=BA\cos\theta$$

2. **Differentiate using angular speed.**

   $$|\mathcal E|=NBA\omega|\sin\theta|$$

3. **Substitute.**

   $$|\mathcal E|=(100)(0.20)(0.010)(50)\sin30^\circ=5.00\,\mathrm V$$

4. **Check the angle.** Use the angle to the normal. The maximum occurs at a normal angle of 90°.

5. **State the result.**

   $$5\,\mathrm V$$

**Answer:** $5\,\mathrm V$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$10\,\mathrm V$** — This gives the peak emf, not the value at 30°.
- **$8.66025\,\mathrm V$** — This uses cosine for the derivative of the flux.
- **$0.1\,\mathrm V$** — This omits the angular speed factor.

#### Situation 8: Mechanical power in a sliding rod

A 0.40 m conducting rod slides at constant 3.0 m/s on ideal rails in a uniform 0.50 T field. The rod, velocity, and field are mutually perpendicular. The closed circuit has total resistance 2.0 Ω. Ignore friction and self-inductance. Find the external mechanical power needed.

1. **Find motional emf.**

   $$\mathcal E=BLv=(0.50)(0.40)(3.0)=0.60\,\mathrm V$$

2. **Find the current.**

   $$I=\mathcal E/R=0.60/2.0=0.30\,\mathrm A$$

3. **Find magnetic drag and balance it.**

   $$F_{\mathrm{ext}}=BIL=(0.50)(0.30)(0.40)=0.060\,\mathrm N$$

4. **Calculate mechanical power.**

   $$P=F_{\mathrm{ext}}v=(0.060)(3.0)=0.180\,\mathrm W$$

5. **Check electrical heating.**

   $$I^2R=(0.30)^2(2.0)=0.180\,\mathrm W$$

6. **State the result.**

   $$0.18\,\mathrm W$$

**Answer:** $0.18\,\mathrm W$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$0.6\,\mathrm W$** — This is the numerical emf, not the mechanical power.
- **$0.06\,\mathrm W$** — This is the numerical drag-force magnitude, not power.
- **$0.36\,\mathrm W$** — This misses the resistance when calculating power.

#### Situation 9: An RL circuit reaches a chosen current

A 12 V ideal source is connected to a series 6.0 Ω resistor and 3.0 H ideal inductor. The initial current is zero. Find the time when current first reaches 75% of its final value.

1. **Calculate the final current and time constant.**

   $$I_\infty=12/6=2.0\,\mathrm A,\quad\tau=L/R=3/6=0.50\,\mathrm s$$

2. **Use the growth solution.**

   $$i/I_\infty=1-e^{-t/\tau}$$

3. **Isolate the remaining fraction.**

   $$e^{-t/\tau}=1-0.75=0.25$$

4. **Take logarithms.**

   $$t=-0.50\ln0.25=0.50\ln4$$

5. **Check the time.** Reaching 75% takes more than one time constant.

6. **State the result.**

   $$0.693147\,\mathrm s$$

**Answer:** $0.693147\,\mathrm s$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$0.5\,\mathrm s$** — One time constant gives about 63.2% of the final current.
- **$0.143841\,\mathrm s$** — This confuses the remaining fraction with the reached fraction.
- **$1.5\,\mathrm s$** — A linear current-rise model does not apply.

#### Situation 10: Transformer input current under load

An ideal transformer receives 24 V rms at its 100-turn primary. Its 500-turn secondary supplies a 240 Ω resistor. Use sinusoidal steady operation. Find the primary rms current.

1. **Find the secondary rms voltage.**

   $$V_s=V_pN_s/N_p=24(500/100)=120\,\mathrm V$$

2. **Find secondary current.**

   $$I_s=V_s/R=120/240=0.50\,\mathrm A$$

3. **Use ideal power conservation.**

   $$V_pI_p=V_sI_s$$

4. **Solve the primary current.**

   $$I_p=(120)(0.50)/24=2.50\,\mathrm A$$

5. **Check power.**

   $$P_{\mathrm{in}}=24(2.50)=60\,\mathrm W=P_{\mathrm{load}}$$

6. **State the result.**

   $$2.5\,\mathrm A$$

**Answer:** $2.5\,\mathrm A$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$0.5\,\mathrm A$** — This is the secondary current.
- **$0.1\,\mathrm A$** — This applies the load directly to the primary voltage.
- **$12.5\,\mathrm A$** — This applies the turns ratio twice to the primary current.

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$I=2+(0.3-2)e^{-0.3/0.5}$$

6. **Check the physical result.** The current lies between its initial value and the source-defined final value.

7. **State the result.**

   $$1.06702\,\mathrm A$$

**Answer:** $1.06702\,\mathrm A$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$0.902377\,\mathrm A$** — This assumes zero initial current.
- **$0.164643\,\mathrm A$** — This ignores the connected source.

<h4 id="solution-m9h21">Situation 21: Where the inductor energy goes · Find the current at the stated time</h4>

An inductor of 1.8 H initially carries 1.5 A. It discharges into a closed 9 Ω resistor loop with no source. Neglect other losses. Consider time 0.08 s after the decay begins. The current reference follows the initial current. Find the current at the stated time.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Current decays with time constant L/R. Energy depends on current squared, so it decays twice as fast in the exponential.

2. **Find the current decay.**

   $$I=I_0e^{-Rt/L}=1.00548\,\mathrm A$$

3. **Find the remaining and dissipated energies.**

   $$U_L=\tfrac12LI_0^2e^{-2Rt/L},\quad Q_R=\tfrac12LI_0^2-U_L$$

4. **Derive the energy half-time.**

   $$e^{-2Rt_{1/2}/L}=1/2\ \Rightarrow\ t_{1/2}=\frac{L\ln2}{2R}$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$I=1.5e^{-9(0.08)/1.8}$$

6. **Check the physical result.** The current remains in its original direction while its magnitude decays.

7. **State the result.**

   $$1.00548\,\mathrm A$$

**Answer:** $1.00548\,\mathrm A$

   $$I_p=P_{\rm in}/V_p$$

**Why the other answers do not work:**
