### Worked applications: models, calculations, and interpretation

These worked examples apply the module concepts. Each example states its conditions, explains the model, and shows the complete working.

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m1h1">Worked application 1: A tether below a buoyant body</h4>

A sealed rigid body is completely submerged. Buoyancy exceeds its weight. A string attached to the bottom holds it at rest.

The force balance determines the tension in the bottom tether.

1. **Identify all forces.** The bottom tether pulls downward. Weight is downward; buoyancy is upward.

2. **Choose upward positive.**

   $$\sum F_y=F_B-mg-T=0$$

3. **Rearrange.**

   $$T=F_B-mg>0$$

4. **Check the geometry.** An upper string could not provide this required downward force. Attachment location is part of the problem.

**Result:** $T=F_B-mg$

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m1h2">Worked application 2: A stone leaves a floating boat</h4>

A dense stone is carried in a boat floating on a still-water tank. The stone is then placed on the tank bottom. The boat stays afloat. Ignore absorption and volume changes.

The comparison determines the water-level change when the stone moves from the floating boat to the tank bottom.

1. **Count the stone’s contribution while carried.**

   $$V_{\mathrm{before}}=\frac{m_s}{\rho_w}$$

2. **Count it after sinking.**

   $$V_{\mathrm{after}}=\frac{m_s}{\rho_s}$$

3. **Use the density condition.**

   $$\rho_s>\rho_w\ \Longrightarrow\ V_{\mathrm{after}}<V_{\mathrm{before}}$$

4. **Interpret the tank.** The boat’s own weight contribution remains. The reduced total displacement lowers the level.

**Result:** It decreases.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m1h3">Worked application 3: Depth and buoyancy</h4>

The same rigid sealed block is moved deeper while remaining fully submerged in an incompressible liquid of constant density. Its volume does not change.

The comparison explains buoyancy at different depths for the same fully submerged rigid block.

1. **Compare top and bottom pressures.**

   $$P_b-P_t=\rho g(h_b-h_t)$$

2. **Hold the relevant quantities fixed.** The block’s height, volume, and fluid density do not change.

3. **Use displaced volume.**

   $$F_B=\rho gV_{\mathrm{disp}}$$

4. **State the limit.** A compressible body or a liquid whose density changes with depth needs a different analysis.

**Result:** It remains unchanged.

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m1h4">Worked application 4: Pressure across a hatch</h4>

A small flat hatch is horizontal, so all its points have the same depth. Water is on one side and a pressurized gas is on the other.

The pressure difference across the hatch determines its net force.

1. **Choose a force direction.** Take the water-to-gas direction as positive.

2. **Add both signed forces.**

   $$F_{\mathrm{net}}=+P_wA-P_gA$$

3. **Check the sign.** A negative result means the net force points from the gas side toward the water.

4. **Check the pressure model.** For a tall vertical hatch, pressure varies with depth; a single-depth value cannot be applied to the whole hatch without justification.

**Result:** The pressure difference across the hatch: $F_{\mathrm{net}}=(P_w-P_g)A$.

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m1h5">Worked application 5: Hydraulics: diameter is not area</h4>

An ideal incompressible hydraulic lift has an output piston with three times the input piston’s diameter. Ignore piston weights and height differences.

The piston area ratio connects the output force and travel to the input force and travel.

1. **Convert diameter ratio to area ratio.**

   $$\frac{A_2}{A_1}=\left(\frac{D_2}{D_1}\right)^2=9$$

2. **Apply equal pressure.**

   $$\frac{F_1}{A_1}=\frac{F_2}{A_2}$$

3. **Apply volume conservation.**

   $$A_1d_1=A_2d_2$$

4. **Check ideal work.**

   $$F_1d_1=F_2d_2$$

**Result:** Force ×9; travel ÷9.

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m1h6">Worked application 6: Two fluid layers and a hatch</h4>

An open tank has a 0.30 m-thick oil layer above water. A small horizontal hatch is 0.40 m below the oil–water interface. Both the top liquid surface and the outside of the hatch are at the same atmospheric pressure. Oil density is 800 kg/m³; water density is 1000 kg/m³; hatch area is 0.020 m². The calculation determines the net water-side force magnitude. Use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

1. **Choose the pressure reference.** The matching atmospheric terms cancel across the hatch.

2. **Add the two hydrostatic contributions.**

   $$\Delta P=\rho_o gh_o+\rho_w gh_w$$

3. **Substitute layer by layer.**

   $$\Delta P=800(9.81)(0.30)+1000(9.81)(0.40)=6278.4\,\mathrm{Pa}$$

4. **Convert pressure difference into force.** Both layers contribute to the pressure at the hatch.

   $$F=\Delta PA=6278.4(0.020)$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$125.568\,\mathrm N$$

**Result:** $125.568\,\mathrm N$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m1h7">Worked application 7: A floating block with cargo</h4>

A rigid block has volume 0.010 m³ and average density 600 kg/m³. It floats in freshwater of density 1000 kg/m³. A 2.0 kg load is placed on top without touching the water. The calculation determines the fraction of the block volume submerged after loading.

1. **Find the block mass.**

   $$m_b=\rho_bV=600(0.010)=6.0\,\mathrm{kg}$$

2. **Balance the full supported weight.**

   $$\rho_wgV_{\mathrm{sub}}=(m_b+m_L)g$$

3. **Find displaced volume.**

   $$V_{\mathrm{sub}}=\frac{6.0+2.0}{1000}=0.0080\,\mathrm{m^3}$$

4. **Divide by the block volume.** The result is below one, so the stated floating state is possible.

   $$f=\frac{0.0080}{0.010}=0.80$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$0.8$$

**Result:** $0.8$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m1h8">Worked application 8: A bottom-tether force</h4>

A sealed 0.0040 m³ body has mass 2.0 kg and is fully submerged in freshwater. A tether to the bottom holds it at rest. Water density is 1000 kg/m³. The calculation determines the tether tension magnitude using $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

1. **Find buoyancy.**

   $$F_B=1000(9.81)(0.0040)=39.24\,\mathrm N$$

2. **Find weight.**

   $$mg=2.0(9.81)=19.62\,\mathrm N$$

3. **Apply upward-positive equilibrium.**

   $$F_B-mg-T=0$$

4. **Isolate the downward tether magnitude.** A positive downward tension is consistent with a body that would otherwise rise.

   $$T=39.24-19.62$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$19.62\,\mathrm N$$

**Result:** $19.62\,\mathrm N$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m1h9">Worked application 9: Hydraulic force and displacement</h4>

An ideal lift uses piston diameters 2.0 cm and 10.0 cm. An input force of 120 N moves the small piston down 0.25 m. Ignore weights and elevation differences. The calculation determines the rise of the large piston.

1. **Find the area ratio.**

   $$\frac{A_2}{A_1}=\left(\frac{10.0}{2.0}\right)^2=25$$

2. **Use displaced-volume equality.**

   $$A_1d_1=A_2d_2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d_2=\frac{d_1}{25}$$

3. **Substitute the input travel.** The force is multiplied by 25, but the travel is divided by 25.

   $$d_2=\frac{0.25}{25}$$

4. **Check work consistency.**

   $$F_2=3000\,\mathrm N,\quad 120(0.25)=3000(0.010)=30\,\mathrm J$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$0.01\,\mathrm m$$

**Result:** $0.01\,\mathrm m$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m1h10">Worked application 10: Apparent weight identifies density</h4>

A uniform object weighs 30 N in air and has an apparent weight of 18 N while fully submerged in water. Ignore air buoyancy. Water density is 1000 kg/m³. The calculation determines the object’s density.

1. **Identify the buoyant force.**

   $$F_B=W-T=30-18=12\,\mathrm N$$

2. **Write weight and buoyancy for the same volume.**

   $$W=\rho_oVg,\qquad F_B=\rho_wVg$$

3. **Eliminate volume and gravity.**

   $$\rho_o=\rho_w\frac{W}{W-T}$$

4. **Substitute.** The density exceeds water’s density, consistent with needing an upward support string.

   $$\rho_o=1000\frac{30}{12}$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$2500\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$$

**Result:** $2500\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$

</section>

<h4 id="solution-m1h11">Worked application 11: Two-liquid pressure measurement · the gas gauge pressure</h4>

A stationary U-tube connects a gas to the atmosphere. Following a horizontal-bottom pressure balance gives $P_g+\rho_o gh_o=P_{\rm atm}+\rho_w gh_w$. The oil-side height is $h_o=0.2\,\mathrm m$; the water-side height is $h_w=0.35\,\mathrm m$. Use $\rho_o=800\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, $\rho_w=1000\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$, $P_{\rm atm}=101300\,\mathrm{Pa}$, and $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. The gas acts on a small piston of area $A=0.02\,\mathrm{m^2}$, with atmosphere outside. The calculation determines the gas gauge pressure.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** The liquids are stationary. Add pressure when moving downward and subtract it when moving upward.

2. **Balance pressure at the same bottom level.**

   $$P_g-P_{\rm atm}=g(\rho_wh_w-\rho_oh_o)$$

3. **Substitute the two liquid contributions.**

   $$p=(9.81)[1000(0.35)-800(0.2)]=1863.9\,\mathrm{Pa}$$

4. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$p=1863.9\,\mathrm{Pa}$$

5. **Check the physical result.** The water-side head is larger, so gas pressure exceeds atmospheric pressure.

6. **State the result.**

   $$1863.9\,\mathrm{Pa}$$

**Result:** $1863.9\,\mathrm{Pa}$

</section>

<h4 id="solution-m1h21">Worked application 21: A block across a liquid interface · the total buoyant force</h4>

A rigid block of volume $V=0.004\,\mathrm{m^3}$ and mass $m=3.6\,\mathrm{kg}$ straddles two deep immiscible liquids. The upper liquid has density $800\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$; the lower liquid has density $1000\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$. Fraction $f=0.3$ of the block volume lies in the lower liquid. The rest lies in the upper liquid. An upper support holds the block at rest. Ignore surface tension; use $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. The calculation determines the total buoyant force.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Add buoyancy from each displaced liquid. The upper support supplies any remaining upward force.

2. **Add the two displaced-fluid weights.**

   $$F_B=gV[\rho_1(1-f)+\rho_2f]=33.7464\,\mathrm N$$

3. **Balance all vertical forces.**

   $$T+F_B-mg=0\ \Rightarrow\ T=mg-F_B$$

4. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$F_B=(9.81)(0.004)[800(1-0.3)+1000(0.3)]$$

5. **Check the physical result.** Each liquid contributes through the volume that the block actually displaces.

6. **State the result.**

   $$33.7464\,\mathrm N$$

**Result:** $33.7464\,\mathrm N$

</section>

Use water density $1000\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ and $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$. A third liquid has density $800\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$. The calculation determines the unknown-liquid density.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** The apparent-weight loss equals buoyancy. The same rigid object displaces the same volume in each liquid.

2. **Find water buoyancy from the two readings.**

   $$F_{B,w}=W-T_w=24-16=8\,\mathrm N$$

3. **Infer the volume.**

   $$V=\frac{W-T_w}{\rho_wg}=8.15494\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m^3}$$

4. **Keep weight and mass distinct.**

   $$m=W/g,\quad \rho_b=m/V$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$\rho_x=\rho_w\frac{W-T_x}{W-T_w}=1000\frac{6}{8}$$

6. **Check the physical result.** The larger apparent weight in the unknown liquid means it is less dense than water.

7. **State the result.**

   $$750\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$$

**Result:** $750\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$

A uniform rectangular raft has volume 0.4 m³, horizontal top area 1 m², and material density $400\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$. It carries a 100 kg load in freshwater of density $1000\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$. 1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** A freely floating raft displaces water whose mass equals the raft-plus-load mass.

2. **Include the raft mass.**

   $$m_{\rm total}=\rho_rV+m_L=400(0.4)+100=260\,\mathrm{kg}$$

3. **Use the floating-force balance.**

   $$\rho_wgV_{\rm disp}=m_{\rm total}g\ \Rightarrow\ V_{\rm disp}=0.26\,\mathrm{m^3}$$

4. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$f=V_{\rm disp}/V=0.26/0.4$$

5. **Check the physical result.** The fraction is below one, so the stated dry-top floating condition is possible.

6. **State the result.**

   $$0.65$$
