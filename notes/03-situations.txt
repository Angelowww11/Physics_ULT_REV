### Worked applications: models, calculations, and interpretation

These worked examples apply the module concepts. Each example states its conditions, explains the model, and shows the complete working.

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m3h1">Worked application 1: Contact between identical conductors</h4>

Two identical small conducting spheres carry +8 nC and −2 nC. They touch in isolation and are then separated far enough for the point-charge model.

Charge conservation determines the final charges and their electric interaction after separation.

1. **Conserve charge.**

   $$Q_{\mathrm{total}}=8-2=+6\,\mathrm{nC}$$

2. **Use the identical-sphere condition.**

   $$q_1'=q_2'=\frac{6}{2}=+3\,\mathrm{nC}$$

3. **Determine the force type.** The separated spheres have charges of the same sign, so their mutual force is repulsive.

4. **Keep the condition.** Equal final charges do not follow for arbitrary unequal conductors without further geometric information.

**Result:** They repel, with +3 nC on each sphere.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m3h2">Worked application 2: Zero force with a negative test charge</h4>

At a point, two source-charge forces on a nonzero positive test charge cancel. The test charge is replaced by a negative charge of twice the magnitude without changing the sources or position.

Superposition determines the new net force after the positive test charge becomes a negative charge with twice its magnitude.

1. **Factor out the test charge.**

   $$\vec F_{\mathrm{net}}=q\sum_i\vec E_i$$

2. **Use the original cancellation.**

   $$q\ne0,\quad\vec F_{\mathrm{net}}=0\ \Longrightarrow\ \sum_i\vec E_i=0$$

3. **Replace the test charge.**

   $$\vec F_{\mathrm{new}}=(-2q)\sum_i\vec E_i=0$$

4. **Separate equilibrium from stability.** Zero net force at a point does not by itself establish stable equilibrium.

**Result:** Zero.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m3h3">Worked application 3: Forces in an isolated pair</h4>

Two isolated interacting charges have different masses. Their mutual electric forces have equal magnitudes.

Newton’s third law relates the mutual forces; the different masses determine the accelerations.

1. **Identify the interaction pair.**

   $$\vec F_{12}=-\vec F_{21}$$

2. **Apply the separate equations of motion.**

   $$a_1=F/m_1,\qquad a_2=F/m_2$$

3. **Compare accelerations.**

   $$\frac{a_1}{a_2}=\frac{m_2}{m_1}$$

4. **Do not add forces on different bodies as one target force.** The system’s internal-force sum cancels, but each body can accelerate.

**Result:** The smaller mass has the larger acceleration magnitude.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m3h4">Worked application 4: Superposition with geometry</h4>

A student adds the magnitudes of two perpendicular electric forces on a target charge.

Vector addition combines the perpendicular electric forces without adding their magnitudes directly.

1. **Draw each source-to-target direction.** Charge signs determine attraction or repulsion along each relevant line.

2. **Add components on the target.**

   $$F_x=\sum_iF_{ix},\qquad F_y=\sum_iF_{iy}$$

3. **Recover magnitude and direction.**

   $$F=\sqrt{F_x^2+F_y^2}$$

4. **Choose the angle quadrant from both components.** A calculator’s inverse tangent alone can lose quadrant information.

**Result:** Resolve and add vector components before finding the resultant magnitude.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m3h5">Worked application 5: Grounding sequence in induction</h4>

A negative rod is held near an initially neutral conductor connected to ground. The ground connection is removed first, then the rod is moved away.

The grounding sequence determines the conductor’s final net charge.

1. **Apply the field while grounded.** Electrons can flow to the ground because the negative rod repels them.

2. **Disconnect the ground with the rod still present.** The conductor is left with an electron deficit.

3. **Remove the rod.** Charge redistributes over the conductor without restoring the missing electrons.

4. **Check the alternative sequence.** Removing the rod while still grounded would allow charge to return and give a different result.

**Result:** Positive.

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m3h6">Worked application 6: Two perpendicular source forces</h4>

A +1.0 nC target is at the origin. A −2.0 nC source is at (0.030 m,0), and a −3.0 nC source is at (0,0.040 m). The calculation determines the net-force magnitude on the target. Use $k=8.99\times10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2\,C^{-2}}$.

1. **Set directions.** Both sources attract the positive target: the first force is east, and the second is north.

2. **Calculate the east component.**

   $$F_x=\frac{k(1.0\times10^{-9})(2.0\times10^{-9})}{0.030^2}$$

3. **Calculate the north component.**

   $$F_y=\frac{k(1.0\times10^{-9})(3.0\times10^{-9})}{0.040^2}$$

4. **Combine perpendicular components.** The resultant lies in the northeast quadrant; its magnitude is smaller than the sum of the two positive component magnitudes.

   $$F=\sqrt{F_x^2+F_y^2}$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$2.6139\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm N$$

**Result:** $2.6139\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm N$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m3h7">Worked application 7: Contact followed by separation</h4>

Identical conducting spheres initially carry +8.0 nC and −2.0 nC. They touch in isolation, then their centers are separated by 0.20 m, much larger than their radii. The calculation determines the final force magnitude using $k=8.99\times10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2\,C^{-2}}$.

1. **Conserve the total charge.**

   $$Q=+8.0-2.0=+6.0\,\mathrm{nC}$$

2. **Share equally between identical spheres.**

   $$q_1'=q_2'=+3.0\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm C$$

3. **Use the final charges and final separation.**

   $$F=\frac{(8.99\times10^9)(3.0\times10^{-9})^2}{(0.20)^2}$$

4. **Interpret the direction.** The final charges are both positive, so the force is repulsive.

5. **State the result.**

   $$2.02275\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm N$$

**Result:** $2.02275\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm N$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m3h8">Worked application 8: A cancellation point between like charges</h4>

Fixed charges +4.0 nC and +9.0 nC lie at $x=0$ and $x=0.50\,\mathrm m$. A nonzero test charge is placed between them. The calculation determines the x-coordinate between the sources where the test charge has zero net electric force.

1. **Identify the allowed cancellation region.** Between like charges, the two source forces on a given test charge point oppositely.

2. **Set force magnitudes equal and cancel common factors.**

   $$\frac4{x^2}=\frac9{(0.50-x)^2}$$

3. **Take the positive distance square root.**

   $$\frac2x=\frac3{0.50-x}$$

4. **Solve the linear equation.** The point is closer to the smaller +4 nC source, as required.

   $$2(0.50-x)=3x\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=0.20\,\mathrm m$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$0.2\,\mathrm m$$

**Result:** $0.2\,\mathrm m$

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m3h9">Worked application 9: Tracking electrons through a transfer</h4>

A neutral small object loses $2.0\times10^{10}$ electrons. It then gains $5.0\times10^{9}$ electrons from another object. The calculation determines its final charge. Use $e=1.602\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$.

1. **Count the final electron deficit.**

   $$N_{\mathrm{deficit}}=2.0\times10^{10}-5.0\times10^9=1.5\times10^{10}$$

2. **Choose the charge sign.** The object still has fewer electrons than its neutral state, so its charge is positive.

3. **Multiply the net count by elementary charge.** Track the signed net transfer before multiplying; do not add all transfer magnitudes.

   $$Q=+(1.5\times10^{10})(1.602\times10^{-19})$$

4. **State the result.**

   $$2.403\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm C$$

**Result:** $2.403\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm C$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m3h10">Worked application 10: A mutual force and one acceleration</h4>

Two isolated small charges +2.0 μC and −3.0 μC are 0.30 m apart. The +2.0 μC body has mass 0.010 kg. The calculation determines its initial acceleration magnitude from the electric interaction. Ignore every other force; $k=8.99\times10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2\,C^{-2}}$.

1. **Find the initial force magnitude.**

   $$F=\frac{k|q_1q_2|}{r^2}$$

2. **Convert both microcoulomb values.**

   $$F=\frac{(8.99\times10^9)(2.0\times10^{-6})(3.0\times10^{-6})}{0.30^2}$$

3. **Apply Newton’s second law to the specified body.**

   $$a=\frac F{0.010}$$

4. **Interpret the instantaneous result.** The positive body initially accelerates toward the negative body. The acceleration changes if their separation changes.

5. **State the result.**

   $$59.9333\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$$

**Result:** $59.9333\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$

</section>

<h4 id="solution-m3h11">Worked application 11: Opposing forces on a target charge · the signed net x force on the target</h4>

A positive target charge $q=2\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm C$ of mass $1\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{kg}$ is at the origin. Fixed positive charges $q_A=4.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ and $q_B=9.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ lie at $x=-0.06\,\mathrm m$ and $x=+0.15\,\mathrm m$. Choose right as positive. Use $k=8.99\times10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2\,C^{-2}}$. Ignore all other forces. The calculation determines the signed net x force on the target.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Both sources repel the positive target. Their forces oppose because the sources lie on opposite sides.

2. **Calculate the rightward force from the left source.**

   $$F_A=kqq_A/a^2=1.99778\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm N$$

3. **Calculate the leftward force from the right source.**

   $$F_B=kqq_B/b^2=7.192\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm N$$

4. **Add signed forces.**

   $$F_x=F_A-F_B=1.27858\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm N$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$F_x=1.99778\times10^{-5}-7.192\times10^{-6}$$

6. **Check the physical result.** The sign identifies the physical direction; the two force magnitudes must not simply be added.

7. **State the result.**

   $$1.27858\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm N$$

**Result:** $1.27858\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm N$

</section>

<h4 id="solution-m3h21">Worked application 21: An equilateral three-charge arrangement · the signed net y force on the target</h4>

Two fixed charges $+Q$ occupy the lower corners of an equilateral triangle of side 0.1 m. A target $-q$ at the top has mass $2\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{kg}$. Use $Q=2\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm C$, $q=3\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm C$, and $k=8.99\times10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2\,C^{-2}}$. The base is horizontal; upward is positive y. Ignore gravity and other forces. The calculation determines the signed net y force on the target.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** The two attractions have equal magnitudes. Their horizontal components cancel; their downward vertical components add.

2. **Calculate either source-to-target force magnitude.**

   $$F_0=kQq/a^2=5.394\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm N$$

3. **Resolve the forces using the triangle geometry.**

   $$F_y=-2F_0\cos30^\circ=-\sqrt3F_0=-9.34268\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm N,\quad F_x=0$$

4. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$F_y=-\sqrt3(5.394\times10^{-6})$$

5. **Check the physical result.** The net force lies along the triangle altitude and points downward.

6. **State the result.**

   $$-9.34268\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm N$$

**Result:** $-9.34268\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm N$

</section>

7. **State the result.**

   $$129.424\,{}^\circ$$

**Result:** $129.424\,{}^\circ$

<h4 id="solution-m3h60">Worked application 60: Forces from three square corners · model and interpretation</h4>

A target $+q=2\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm C$ is at $(0,0)$. Fixed sources are $+Q$ at $(a,0)$, $-2Q$ at $(0,a)$, and $+Q$ at $(a,a)$. Use $a=0.08\,\mathrm m$, $Q=4\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm C$, and $k=8.99\times10^9$ in SI units. Choose right and upward as positive x and y.

The diagonal source-to-target distance differs from the square side length.

1. **Identify what stays fixed.** Add the three forces on the same target. The diagonal source is farther away by a factor of square root two.

2. **Define an adjacent-corner force scale.**

   $$K=kqQ/a^2=1.12375\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm N$$

3. **Resolve the diagonal repulsion.**

   $$F_{d,x}=F_{d,y}=-\frac{K}{2\sqrt2}$$

4. **Add both Cartesian components.**

   $$F_x=-K-\frac{K}{2\sqrt2}=-1.52106\times10^{-5},\quad F_y=2K-\frac{K}{2\sqrt2}=1.85019\times10^{-5}\quad(\mathrm N)$$

5. **Apply the conclusion.** The component projection then adds another factor of $1/\sqrt2$.

**Result:** The diagonal distance is $a\sqrt2$, so its inverse-square force has an extra factor of one half.
