### Worked applications: models, calculations, and interpretation

These worked examples apply the module concepts. Each example states its conditions, explains the model, and shows the complete working.

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m2h1">Worked application 1: A hole in an expanding plate</h4>

An unconstrained isotropic metal plate contains a circular hole. It is heated uniformly and remains solid.

The expansion model describes the circular hole to first order.

1. **Identify the geometry.** Uniform isotropic free expansion scales every coordinate separation.

2. **Apply the length factor to the opening.**

   $$D_f=D_0(1+\alpha\Delta T)$$

3. **Separate the measures.** A hole’s area changes by approximately 2αΔT, while its diameter changes by αΔT.

4. **Respect the assumptions.** Constraints, nonuniform heating, or anisotropy need additional modeling.

**Result:** Its diameter increases by the same fractional linear expansion as the plate.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m2h2">Worked application 2: Not enough heat to melt all the ice</h4>

Ice and water exchange heat in an insulated container. The warm water can reach 0°C but cannot supply enough heat to melt all the ice. Assume ordinary pressure and equilibrium.

The energy balance identifies the equilibrium phase and temperature when some ice remains.

1. **Test the phase-change budget first.** Calculate the energy that cooling the warm part to 0°C can release.

2. **Compare with the melting requirement.**

   $$Q_{\mathrm{available}}<m_iL_f$$

3. **Choose the physical regime.** Only part of the ice melts. Both phases remain at 0°C under the stated conditions.

4. **Avoid an invalid algebraic answer.** Do not assume complete melting and accept a final temperature inconsistent with the assumed phase.

**Result:** A mixture of ice and water at 0°C.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m2h3">Worked application 3: Heat is not internal energy</h4>

A gas is compressed adiabatically by an external agent. The agent does positive work on the gas.

The first law connects positive work on the gas to its internal-energy change during adiabatic compression.

1. **Define the work convention.** Use positive Wby for work done by the gas.

2. **Translate compression into signs.**

   $$Q=0,\qquad W_{\mathrm{by}}<0$$

3. **Apply the first law.**

   $$\Delta U=Q-W_{\mathrm{by}}=-W_{\mathrm{by}}>0$$

4. **Interpret the transfer.** Adiabatic means no heat transfer; it does not mean no energy transfer.

**Result:** Its internal energy increases, although no heat enters.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m2h4">Worked application 4: Container and liquid both expand</h4>

A vessel is initially full of liquid. Both are heated uniformly. The liquid’s volume-expansion coefficient equals the vessel’s internal volume-expansion coefficient.

The small-expansion model compares the liquid volume with the vessel capacity after heating.

1. **Write the competing volume changes.**

   $$\Delta V_l=\beta_lV_0\Delta T,\qquad\Delta V_c=\beta_cV_0\Delta T$$

2. **Subtract capacity growth.**

   $$V_{\mathrm{overflow}}\approx V_0(\beta_l-\beta_c)\Delta T$$

3. **Use the stated equality.** The first-order difference is zero.

4. **State the approximation.** This result does not assert that every higher-order effect vanishes.

**Result:** No first-order overflow occurs.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m2h5">Worked application 5: A completed thermodynamic cycle</h4>

A system returns to exactly its initial thermodynamic state after several processes.

The cycle balance relates total heat and total work by the system when the internal energy returns to its initial value.

1. **Use the returned state.** Internal energy has the same final and initial values.

2. **Apply the first law to the complete cycle.**

   $$0=\Delta U_{\mathrm{cycle}}=Q_{\mathrm{net}}-W_{\mathrm{by,net}}$$

3. **Rearrange.**

   $$Q_{\mathrm{net}}=W_{\mathrm{by,net}}$$

4. **Keep net and individual quantities separate.** A nonzero heat or work transfer on one leg is compatible with the final state returning.

**Result:** $Q_{\mathrm{net}}=W_{\mathrm{by,net}}$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m2h6">Worked application 6: Ice mixing: choose the regime first</h4>

In an insulated container of negligible heat capacity, 0.050 kg of ice at 0°C is added to 0.200 kg of water at 40°C. Use $c_w=4186\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ and $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}$. The calculation determines the final equilibrium temperature in °C.

1. **Check available cooling energy.**

   $$Q_{w\to0}=0.200(4186)(40)=33488\,\mathrm J$$

2. **Check full-melting energy.**

   $$Q_{\mathrm{melt}}=0.050(334000)=16700\,\mathrm J$$

3. **Choose the regime.** The available heat exceeds the melting requirement, so all ice melts and the final temperature is above 0°C.

4. **Write the full energy balance.**

   $$0.200(4186)(40-T_f)=16700+0.050(4186)T_f$$

5. **Solve after collecting both water heat capacities.** The result lies between 0°C and the initial warm-water temperature.

   $$T_f=\frac{33488-16700}{(0.200+0.050)(4186)}$$

6. **State the result.**

   $$16.042\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$$

**Result:** $16.042\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m2h7">Worked application 7: Several thermal stages</h4>

The calculation determines the heat needed to turn 0.050 kg of ice at −10°C into water at 20°C. Use $c_i=2100\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$, $c_w=4186\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$, and $L_f=334000\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}$. Ignore losses.

1. **Warm the ice to its melting point.**

   $$Q_1=0.050(2100)[0-(-10)]=1050\,\mathrm J$$

2. **Melt the ice at fixed temperature.**

   $$Q_2=0.050(334000)=16700\,\mathrm J$$

3. **Warm the resulting water.**

   $$Q_3=0.050(4186)(20-0)=4186\,\mathrm J$$

4. **Add the successive required inputs.** Use sensible-heat terms for temperature changes and a latent-heat term for melting.

   $$Q=Q_1+Q_2+Q_3=1050+16700+4186$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$21936\,\mathrm J$$

**Result:** $21936\,\mathrm J$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m2h8">Worked application 8: Overflow from a warming vessel</h4>

A vessel is full with 2.00 L of liquid. Its isotropic solid has linear expansion coefficient $1.0\times10^{-5}$ K⁻¹. The liquid’s volume coefficient is $7.0\times10^{-4}$ K⁻¹. Both warm by 50 K. The calculation determines first-order overflow in litres.

1. **Convert the solid coefficient to a volume coefficient.**

   $$\beta_c\approx3\alpha_c=3.0\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

2. **Calculate liquid expansion.**

   $$\Delta V_l=2.00(7.0\times10^{-4})(50)=0.0700\,\mathrm L$$

3. **Calculate capacity expansion.**

   $$\Delta V_c=2.00(3.0\times10^{-5})(50)=0.0030\,\mathrm L$$

4. **Subtract the extra capacity.** The positive difference is liquid that the expanded vessel cannot hold.

   $$V_{\mathrm{overflow}}=0.0700-0.0030$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$0.067\,\mathrm L$$

**Result:** $0.067\,\mathrm L$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m2h9">Worked application 9: Mixing with a calorimeter</h4>

Water of mass 0.200 kg at 80°C is mixed with 0.300 kg at 20°C in a calorimeter initially at 20°C. The calorimeter heat capacity is 40 J/K. Use $c_w=4186\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$, no losses, and no phase change. The calculation determines the final temperature.

1. **Write heat gains and losses with one final temperature.**

   $$0.200c(T_f-80)+0.300c(T_f-20)+40(T_f-20)=0$$

2. **Collect the coefficients of the final temperature.**

   $$T_f=\frac{0.200c(80)+0.300c(20)+40(20)}{0.500c+40}$$

3. **Substitute the specific heat.**

   $$c=4186\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$$

4. **Check the physical result.** The calorimeter also warms, so the final temperature is slightly lower than the 44°C water-only result.

5. **State the result.**

   $$43.5499\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$$

**Result:** $43.5499\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m2h10">Worked application 10: First-law bookkeeping over two legs</h4>

During leg A, a system absorbs 300 J and does 100 J of work. During leg B, it loses 80 J of heat and has 50 J of work done on it. The calculation determines the total internal-energy change.

1. **Choose one work convention.** Use positive work by the system throughout.

2. **Evaluate leg A.**

   $$\Delta U_A=300-100=200\,\mathrm J$$

3. **Translate both signs in leg B.**

   $$Q_B=-80\,\mathrm J,\quad W_{\mathrm{by},B}=-50\,\mathrm J$$

4. **Evaluate leg B.**

   $$\Delta U_B=-80-(-50)=-30\,\mathrm J$$

5. **Add the changes.** Use signs from the chosen convention, rather than changing conventions between legs.

   $$\Delta U_{\mathrm{total}}=200-30$$

6. **State the result.**

   $$170\,\mathrm J$$

**Result:** $170\,\mathrm J$

</section>

<h4 id="solution-m2h11">Worked application 11: A hot metal heats water and a calorimeter · the final equilibrium temperature in °C</h4>

A 0.15 kg metal sample at 90°C enters 0.2 kg of water at 20°C. The calorimeter also starts at 20°C. Use metal specific heat $900\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$, water specific heat $4186\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$, and calorimeter heat capacity $45\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$. The system is insulated; no phase change occurs. The calculation determines the final equilibrium temperature in °C.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Heat lost by the metal equals heat gained by both the water and the calorimeter.

2. **Write the complete energy balance.**

   $$m_mc_m(T_m-T_f)=(m_wc_w+C_c)(T_f-T_w)$$

3. **Solve before inserting numbers.**

   $$T_f=\frac{m_mc_mT_m+(m_wc_w+C_c)T_w}{m_mc_m+m_wc_w+C_c}=29.2902\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$$

4. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$T_f=29.2902\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$$

5. **Check the physical result.** The final temperature lies between the initial temperatures.

6. **State the result.**

   $$29.2902\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$$

**Result:** $29.2902\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$

</section>

   $$T_f=32.1548\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$$

4. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$\dot Q=\frac{30-5}{0.15+1.2}$$

5. **Check the physical result.** Both layers reduce the heat rate relative to using either layer alone.

6. **State the result.**

   $$18.5185\,\mathrm W$$

A flat wall has two layers in series. The hot-side layer has thickness 0.03 m and conductivity $0.20\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$. The cold-side layer has thickness 0.06 m and conductivity $0.050\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$. Area is 1 m². Surface temperatures stay at 30°C and 5°C for 600 s. Assume one-dimensional steady conduction and perfect thermal contact. The calculation determines the energy transferred during the stated time.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** The same heat rate passes through both layers. Add thermal resistances, not conductivities.

2. **Calculate thermal resistances.**

   $$R_1=L_1/(k_1A)=0.15,\quad R_2=L_2/(k_2A)=1.2\quad(\mathrm{K\,W^{-1}})$$

3. **Use the series heat-flow law.**

   $$\dot Q=(T_h-T_c)/(R_1+R_2)=18.5185\,\mathrm W$$

4. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$Q=\dot Qt=18.5185(600)$$

5. **Check the physical result.** Constant steady heat rate multiplied by seconds gives joules.

6. **State the result.**

   $$11111.1\,\mathrm J$$

**Result:** $11111.1\,\mathrm J$

6. **State the result.**

<h4 id="solution-m2h51">Worked application 51: Close a thermodynamic cycle · the signed heat for stage C</h4>

A gas completes a three-stage cycle. Heat entering the gas is positive; work done by the gas is positive. Stage A has $Q_A=600\,\mathrm J$ and $W_A=200\,\mathrm J$. Stage B has $Q_B=-150\,\mathrm J$ and $W_B=-80\,\mathrm J$. Stage C has $W_C=50\,\mathrm J$. The final state equals the initial state. Define engine efficiency as net cycle work divided by total positive heat input. The calculation determines the signed heat for stage C.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Use $\Delta U=Q-W$ for every stage. A closed cycle has zero total internal-energy change.

2. **Calculate the known energy changes.**

   $$\Delta U_A=600-200=400\,\mathrm J,\quad\Delta U_B=-150-(-80)=-70\,\mathrm J$$

3. **Close the internal-energy balance.**

   $$\Delta U_C=-(\Delta U_A+\Delta U_B)=-330\,\mathrm J$$

4. **Recover the missing heat.**

   $$Q_C=\Delta U_C+W_C=-330+50=-280\,\mathrm J$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$Q_C=-280\,\mathrm J$$

6. **Check the physical result.** A negative stage-C heat means energy leaves the gas as heat.

7. **State the result.**

   $$-280\,\mathrm J$$

**Result:** $-280\,\mathrm J$
