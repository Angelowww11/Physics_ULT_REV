### Situational problems: choose the law, then solve

These added practice problems apply the module concepts. They are not copied examination questions. Keep the stated assumptions when you use each result.

#### Situation 1: Dielectric with the battery removed

An ideal charged capacitor is disconnected from its battery. A dielectric completely fills its unchanged gap, increasing capacitance by a factor κ>1. Ignore leakage. Which comparison is correct?

1. **Identify the boundary condition.** Disconnection and negligible leakage hold the free plate charge fixed.

2. **Use the new capacitance.**

   $$C'=\kappa C$$

3. **Derive voltage and energy ratios.**

   $$V'=\frac Q{\kappa C}=\frac V\kappa,\quad U'=\frac{Q^2}{2\kappa C}=\frac U\kappa$$

4. **Account for energy.** Field energy can decrease while the electric force does mechanical work on the inserted material.

**Answer:** $Q$ stays fixed, while $V$ and stored field energy each divide by $\kappa$.

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$V$ stays fixed and charge multiplies by $\kappa$.** — That describes a connected ideal voltage source, not this isolated capacitor.
- **Charge and energy both stay fixed.** — Charge conservation does not imply fixed field energy.
- **Energy multiplies by $\kappa$ because capacitance increases.** — That uses a fixed-voltage energy comparison despite the disconnected battery.

#### Situation 2: Why connected insertion needs the source

A dielectric is inserted quasistatically while an ideal battery stays connected to a capacitor at fixed voltage. Capacitance increases. How can the capacitor’s stored energy increase while the dielectric is pulled inward?

1. **Use the fixed-voltage condition.**

   $$Q=CV,\qquad U=\tfrac12CV^2$$

2. **Compare source work for a change in capacitance.**

   $$W_{\mathrm{battery}}=V\Delta Q=V^2\Delta C$$

3. **Compare the stored-energy increase.**

   $$\Delta U=\tfrac12V^2\Delta C$$

4. **Identify the remaining energy transfer.** In the ideal quasistatic model, the remaining half can appear as mechanical work. A field-only comparison omits this source contribution.

**Answer:** The battery supplies energy; the capacitor field is not an isolated energy system.

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **Energy conservation fails for dielectrics.** — Include the source and mechanical work before testing conservation.
- **The plate charge must stay fixed because the geometry is fixed.** — A connected battery can transfer charge as capacitance changes.
- **A rise in capacitor energy proves the force pushes the dielectric outward.** — That inference omits the battery energy exchange.

#### Situation 3: Parallel capacitors after disconnection

A charged capacitor is disconnected from its source, then connected in parallel with an initially uncharged equal capacitor, with corresponding plates joined. A small connection resistance allows equilibrium. Which statement is correct?

1. **Conserve charge on the joined positive node.**

   $$CV_0=(C+C)V_f$$

2. **Find the shared final voltage.**

   $$V_f=V_0/2$$

3. **Compare field energies.**

   $$U_i=\tfrac12CV_0^2,\quad U_f=\tfrac12(2C)(V_0/2)^2=U_i/2$$

4. **Complete the energy account.** The finite connection dissipates energy during the transient. Conserving charge does not require conserving field energy alone.

**Answer:** Charge is conserved, the final voltage halves, and some initial field energy is dissipated.

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **Both charge and capacitor field energy remain entirely in the final electric field.** — The final shared voltage gives a lower total field energy.
- **The final voltage remains equal to the original voltage.** — No connected battery holds it there.
- **The initially uncharged capacitor cannot acquire charge.** — The joined conductors allow redistribution.

#### Situation 4: Series charge and component voltage

Two initially uncharged capacitors are placed in series across a battery with an initially neutral isolated intermediate node. One capacitor has three times the other’s capacitance. Which comparison is correct?

1. **Use the intermediate-node condition.** Opposite induced charges on the two inner plates have equal magnitudes.

2. **Apply the component relation.**

   $$V_1=Q/C_1,\qquad V_2=Q/C_2$$

3. **Take the ratio.**

   $$\frac{V_{\mathrm{small}}}{V_{\mathrm{large}}}=\frac{C_{\mathrm{large}}}{C_{\mathrm{small}}}=3$$

4. **Distinguish parallel.** Parallel connections fix common voltage instead of common series charge.

**Answer:** They have equal charge magnitudes; the smaller capacitor has three times the voltage.

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **They have equal voltages; the larger has three times the charge.** — That is the parallel condition.
- **The larger capacitor must have the larger voltage.** — Voltage is inversely proportional to capacitance at the common series charge.
- **Their equivalent capacitance exceeds the larger component.** — A positive series equivalent is smaller than the smallest component.

#### Situation 5: Separating isolated capacitor plates

An ideal parallel-plate capacitor is charged and isolated. Its plate gap doubles while overlap area and permittivity remain fixed. Ignore fringing and leakage. Which result follows?

1. **Change the geometry.**

   $$C=\epsilon A/d\quad\Longrightarrow\quad C'=C/2$$

2. **Hold charge fixed.**

   $$V'=Q/C'=2V$$

3. **Use the fixed-charge energy form.**

   $$U'=Q^2/(2C')=2U$$

4. **Check the energy transfer.** The external agent’s positive separation work increases stored field energy.

**Answer:** Capacitance halves, voltage doubles, and field energy doubles.

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **Capacitance doubles because the gap increases.** — Capacitance is inversely proportional to the gap.
- **Voltage stays fixed because it was once connected to a battery.** — A removed battery no longer enforces voltage.
- **Energy halves because capacitance halves.** — That energy scaling would require fixed voltage, not fixed charge.

#### Situation 6: Recovering a series component’s energy

Initially uncharged capacitors 3.0 μF and 6.0 μF are in series across 12 V, with a neutral isolated middle node. Find the energy stored in the 3.0 μF capacitor.

1. **Reduce the series pair.**

   $$C_{\mathrm{eq}}=\frac{3(6)}{3+6}=2\,\mu\mathrm F$$

2. **Find common charge.**

   $$Q=C_{\mathrm{eq}}V=2(12)=24\,\mu\mathrm C$$

3. **Recover the small capacitor’s voltage.**

   $$V_3=Q/C_3=24/3=8\,\mathrm V$$

4. **Calculate that component’s energy.** The 6 μF component has the remaining 4 V, so the recovered voltages add to 12 V.

   $$U_3=\tfrac12(3.0\times10^{-6})(8)^2$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$9.6\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm J$$

**Answer:** $9.6\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm J$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$2.16\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm J$** — This incorrectly puts the full 12 V across the 3 μF component.
- **$4.8\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm J$** — The component energy includes one half, but its actual voltage must first be found.
- **$1.44\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm J$** — This is the total energy of the series combination.

#### Situation 7: Isolated dielectric insertion

A 2.0 μF capacitor is charged to 10 V and disconnected. An ideal dielectric of $\kappa=3$ fully fills the unchanged gap without leakage. Find the final stored energy.

1. **Find the charge before disconnection.**

   $$Q=(2.0\times10^{-6})(10)=20\,\mu\mathrm C$$

2. **Apply the dielectric change.**

   $$C'=3C=6.0\,\mu\mathrm F$$

3. **Hold charge fixed.**

   $$U'=\frac{Q^2}{2C'}=\frac{(20\times10^{-6})^2}{2(6.0\times10^{-6})}$$

4. **Check the comparison.** The initial energy is 100 μJ. Dividing by $\kappa=3$ gives the final field energy.

5. **State the result.**

   $$3.33333\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm J$$

**Answer:** $3.33333\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm J$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$3\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm J$** — This uses the connected fixed-voltage scaling.
- **$1\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm J$** — This assumes unchanged field energy despite the mechanical interaction.
- **$1.11111\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm J$** — This divides the energy by κ² rather than κ at fixed charge.

#### Situation 8: Battery work during dielectric insertion

An ideal battery keeps 12 V across an initially 2.0 μF capacitor. A dielectric increases its capacitance to 8.0 μF during quasistatic insertion. Find the energy supplied by the battery, not just the capacitor-energy change.

1. **Find the capacitance increase.**

   $$\Delta C=(8.0-2.0)\,\mu\mathrm F=6.0\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$$

2. **Find charge delivered at fixed voltage.**

   $$\Delta Q=V\Delta C=12(6.0\times10^{-6})=72\,\mu\mathrm C$$

3. **Calculate source work.**

   $$W_{\mathrm{battery}}=V\Delta Q=12(72\times10^{-6})$$

4. **Check the larger energy account.** The rest can become mechanical work in the stated quasistatic idealization.

   $$\Delta U=\tfrac12\Delta CV^2=432\,\mu\mathrm J$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$8.64\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm J$$

**Answer:** $8.64\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm J$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$4.32\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm J$** — This is the capacitor’s stored-energy increase, half the ideal battery work for this process.
- **$0.001152\,\mathrm J$** — This counts the full final CV² rather than the change in delivered charge.
- **$2.88\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm J$** — This omits the actual capacitance change.

#### Situation 9: Geometry change after isolation

An ideal parallel-plate capacitor has area 0.030 m², gap 1.2 mm, and dielectric constant $\kappa=2$. It is charged to 100 V and isolated. The gap then doubles while the dielectric still completely fills it and κ remains fixed. Find the final stored energy. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{F\,m^{-1}}$.

1. **Convert the original gap.**

   $$d_0=1.2\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm m$$

2. **Find the initial capacitance.**

   $$C_0=\frac{\kappa\epsilon_0A}{d_0}=4.425\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm F$$

3. **Hold the isolated charge fixed as the gap doubles.**

   $$C_f=C_0/2,\qquad Q=C_0V_0$$

4. **Use the final capacitance with that fixed charge.** The final energy is twice the initial energy. External work separates the attracting plates.

   $$U_f=\frac{(C_0V_0)^2}{2(C_0/2)}=C_0V_0^2$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$4.425\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm J$$

**Answer:** $4.425\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm J$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$1.10625\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm J$** — This applies fixed-voltage scaling to an isolated capacitor.
- **$2.2125\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm J$** — This is the initial field energy.
- **$8.85\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm J$** — This doubles the correct final energy again.

#### Situation 10: Unequally charged capacitors share charge

Isolated capacitors 2.0 μF at 10 V and 3.0 μF at 4 V are connected in parallel with corresponding positive plates joined. A small resistance allows equilibrium. Find the final common voltage.

1. **Find charge on the joined positive node.**

   $$Q_{\mathrm{total}}=(2)(10)+(3)(4)=32\,\mu\mathrm C$$

2. **Find the final parallel capacitance.**

   $$C_f=2+3=5\,\mu\mathrm F$$

3. **Divide conserved charge by final capacitance.**

   $$V_f=32/5=6.4\,\mathrm V$$

4. **Check the energy difference.** The transient dissipates 21.6 μJ; conserving charge alone does not keep all initial energy in the final electric field.

   $$U_i=124\,\mu\mathrm J,\quad U_f=102.4\,\mu\mathrm J$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$6.4\,\mathrm V$$

**Answer:** $6.4\,\mathrm V$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$7\,\mathrm V$** — This averages voltages without capacitance weighting.
- **$8\,\mathrm V$** — Conserve the joined-node charge rather than selecting an arbitrary intermediate voltage.
- **$14\,\mathrm V$** — Parallel connection does not add the original voltages.

<h4 id="solution-m6h11">Situation 11: A series capacitor feeds a parallel pair · Find the equivalent capacitance</h4>

An initially uncharged capacitor $C_1=3\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$ connects in series with a parallel pair $C_2=2.0\,\mu\mathrm F$ and $C_3=4\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$. An ideal 12 V source connects across the complete network. The internal connecting node starts neutral. Use the final steady electrostatic state. Find the equivalent capacitance.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Combine the parallel pair first. The series branch charges have equal magnitudes; the parallel capacitors share voltage.

2. **Reduce the parallel pair.**

   $$C_p=C_2+C_3=6\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$$

3. **Reduce the remaining series pair.**

   $$C_{\rm eq}=C_1C_p/(C_1+C_p)=2\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$$

4. **Recover branch charge and voltage.**

   $$Q_1=C_{\rm eq}V=2.4\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm C,\quad V_p=Q_1/C_p=4\,\mathrm V$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$C_{\rm eq}=\frac{3\times10^{-6}(6\times10^{-6})}{9\times10^{-6}}$$

6. **Check the physical result.** The total series capacitance is below either of its two branch capacitances.

7. **State the result.**

   $$2\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$$

**Answer:** $2\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$9\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$** — This treats the whole network as parallel.
- **$9.23077\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm F$** — This treats the parallel pair as series.
- **$6\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$** — This omits the series capacitor.

<h4 id="solution-m6h15">Situation 15: A series capacitor feeds a parallel pair · model check</h4>

An initially uncharged capacitor $C_1=3\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$ connects in series with a parallel pair $C_2=2.0\,\mu\mathrm F$ and $C_3=4\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$. An ideal 12 V source connects across the complete network. The internal connecting node starts neutral. Use the final steady electrostatic state. Which equality applies to the two capacitors in the parallel pair?

1. **Identify what stays fixed.** Combine the parallel pair first. The series branch charges have equal magnitudes; the parallel capacitors share voltage.

2. **Reduce the parallel pair.**

   $$C_p=C_2+C_3=6\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$$

3. **Reduce the remaining series pair.**

   $$C_{\rm eq}=C_1C_p/(C_1+C_p)=2\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$$

4. **Recover branch charge and voltage.**

   $$Q_1=C_{\rm eq}V=2.4\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm C,\quad V_p=Q_1/C_p=4\,\mathrm V$$

5. **Apply the conclusion.** Each connects between the same two nodes.

**Answer:** Their voltages are equal; their charges need not be equal.

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **Their charges must equal the full C1 charge separately.** — Their charges sum to the charge magnitude of the parallel branch.

- **Their capacitances become equal after charging.** — Capacitance is set by geometry and material.

Assume a quasistatic reversible change without leakage or dissipation. Find the final stored energy.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Isolation fixes charge, not voltage. Geometry and dielectric together determine the capacitance ratio.

2. **Calculate the new capacitance ratio.**

   $$\frac{C_f}{C_i}=\kappa\frac{A_f/A_i}{d_f/d_i}=3\frac{1.5}{2.5}$$

3. **Conserve the initial plate charge.**

   $$Q=C_iV_i=2.7\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm C$$

4. **Use charge-constrained voltage and energy.**

   $$V_f=Q/C_f,\quad U_f=Q^2/(2C_f),\quad W_{\rm ext}=U_f-U_i$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$U_f=Q^2/(2C_f)=6.75\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm J$$

6. **Check the physical result.** At fixed charge, increasing capacitance reduces stored field energy.

7. **State the result.**

   $$6.75\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm J$$

**Answer:** $6.75\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm J$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$2.187\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm J$** — This incorrectly keeps the original voltage fixed.
- **$1.215\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm J$** — The field energy changes when capacitance changes at fixed charge.
- **$1.35\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm J$** — This omits the energy one-half.

<h4 id="solution-m6h41">Situation 41: Connect oppositely charged capacitors · Find the signed final common voltage</h4>

Two isolated capacitors have $C_1=3\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$ at 10 V and $C_2=2.0\,\mu\mathrm F$ at 4 V. They connect in parallel with C1’s positive plate joined to C2’s negative plate. The other plates also join. A small resistance allows settling. Define the joined C1-positive node as the positive voltage reference. Find the signed final common voltage.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Conserve signed charge on each joined node. Opposite initial polarities subtract; resistor heating accounts for lost field energy.

2. **Add signed charge on the reference node.**

   $$Q_{\rm node}=C_1V_1-C_2V_2=2.2\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm C$$

3. **Use the final parallel capacitance.**

   $$V_f=Q_{\rm node}/(C_1+C_2)=4.4\,\mathrm V$$

4. **Compare field energies.**

   $$U_i=\tfrac12C_1V_1^2+\tfrac12C_2V_2^2=1.66\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm J,\quad U_f=\tfrac12(C_1+C_2)V_f^2=4.84\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm J$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$V_f=\frac{C_1V_1-C_2V_2}{C_1+C_2}=4.4\,\mathrm V$$

6. **Check the physical result.** The positive result means C1’s initial reference-node charge dominates.

7. **State the result.**

   $$4.4\,\mathrm V$$

**Answer:** $4.4\,\mathrm V$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$7.6\,\mathrm V$** — This joins like polarities instead of the stated opposite polarities.
- **$3\,\mathrm V$** — Voltage averaging needs equal capacitances; signed charge is the general rule.
- **$6\,\mathrm V$** — Parallel settling does not retain the original voltage difference unchanged.

<h4 id="solution-m6h51">Situation 51: Two dielectric layers through the gap · Find the equivalent capacitance</h4>

A parallel-plate capacitor has area 0.02 m² and total gap 0.0015 m. Dielectric 1 of relative permittivity 2 fills fraction 0.5 of the gap thickness. 1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** The field crosses both layers consecutively. They form series capacitances with the same free-charge magnitude per area.

2. **Add the two thickness-dependent inverse capacitances.**

   $$C=\frac{\varepsilon_0A}{d[f/\kappa_1+(1-f)/\kappa_2]}=3.37143\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm F$$

   $$D=Q/A=CV/A=2.02286\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{C\,m^{-2}}$$

4. **Recover each layer’s field and voltage.**

   $$E_1=D/(\kappa_1\varepsilon_0),\quad V_1=E_1fd$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$C=\frac{\varepsilon_0A}{d[0.5/2+(1-0.5)/5]}$$

6. **Check the physical result.** The effective relative permittivity lies between the two material values.

7. **State the result.**

   $$3.37143\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm F$$

**Answer:** $3.37143\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm F$

**Why the other answers do not work:**

- **$4.13\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm F$** — This averages parallel-area permittivities instead of series thickness layers.
- **$1.18\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm F$** — This ignores both dielectrics.
- **$1.18\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm F$** — This multiplies dielectric constants without a series-layer derivation.

<h4 id="solution-m6h55">Situation 55: Two dielectric layers through the gap · model check</h4>
