### Worked applications: models, calculations, and interpretation

These worked examples apply the module concepts. Each example states its conditions, explains the model, and shows the complete working.

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m6h1">Worked application 1: Dielectric with the battery removed</h4>

An ideal charged capacitor is disconnected from its battery. A dielectric completely fills its unchanged gap, increasing capacitance by a factor κ>1. Ignore leakage.

Charge conservation determines the voltage and stored energy after the dielectric enters the isolated capacitor.

1. **Identify the boundary condition.** Disconnection and negligible leakage hold the free plate charge fixed.

2. **Use the new capacitance.**

   $$C'=\kappa C$$

3. **Derive voltage and energy ratios.**

   $$V'=\frac Q{\kappa C}=\frac V\kappa,\quad U'=\frac{Q^2}{2\kappa C}=\frac U\kappa$$

4. **Account for energy.** Field energy can decrease while the electric force does mechanical work on the inserted material.

**Result:** $Q$ stays fixed, while $V$ and stored field energy each divide by $\kappa$.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m6h2">Worked application 2: The source’s role during connected dielectric insertion</h4>

A dielectric is inserted quasistatically while an ideal battery stays connected to a capacitor at fixed voltage. Capacitance increases.

The energy balance includes battery work, increased capacitor energy, and mechanical work during dielectric insertion at fixed voltage.

1. **Use the fixed-voltage condition.**

   $$Q=CV,\qquad U=\tfrac12CV^2$$

2. **Compare source work for a change in capacitance.**

   $$W_{\mathrm{battery}}=V\Delta Q=V^2\Delta C$$

3. **Compare the stored-energy increase.**

   $$\Delta U=\tfrac12V^2\Delta C$$

4. **Identify the remaining energy transfer.** In the ideal quasistatic model, the remaining half can appear as mechanical work. A field-only comparison omits this source contribution.

**Result:** The battery supplies energy; the capacitor field is not an isolated energy system.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m6h3">Worked application 3: Parallel capacitors after disconnection</h4>

A charged capacitor is disconnected from its source, then connected in parallel with an initially uncharged equal capacitor, with corresponding plates joined. A small connection resistance allows equilibrium.

Charge conservation determines the final common voltage; the connection resistance accounts for the field-energy decrease.

1. **Conserve charge on the joined positive node.**

   $$CV_0=(C+C)V_f$$

2. **Find the shared final voltage.**

   $$V_f=V_0/2$$

3. **Compare field energies.**

   $$U_i=\tfrac12CV_0^2,\quad U_f=\tfrac12(2C)(V_0/2)^2=U_i/2$$

4. **Complete the energy account.** The finite connection dissipates energy during the transient. Conserving charge does not require conserving field energy alone.

**Result:** Charge is conserved, the final voltage halves, and some initial field energy is dissipated.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m6h4">Worked application 4: Series charge and component voltage</h4>

Two initially uncharged capacitors are placed in series across a battery with an initially neutral isolated intermediate node. One capacitor has three times the other’s capacitance.

The initially neutral middle node determines the series charge magnitudes and the voltage division between unequal capacitances.

1. **Use the intermediate-node condition.** Opposite induced charges on the two inner plates have equal magnitudes.

2. **Apply the component relation.**

   $$V_1=Q/C_1,\qquad V_2=Q/C_2$$

3. **Take the ratio.**

   $$\frac{V_{\mathrm{small}}}{V_{\mathrm{large}}}=\frac{C_{\mathrm{large}}}{C_{\mathrm{small}}}=3$$

4. **Distinguish parallel.** Parallel connections fix common voltage instead of common series charge.

**Result:** They have equal charge magnitudes; the smaller capacitor has three times the voltage.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m6h5">Worked application 5: Separating isolated capacitor plates</h4>

An ideal parallel-plate capacitor is charged and isolated. Its plate gap doubles while overlap area and permittivity remain fixed. Ignore fringing and leakage.

The isolated-capacitor model determines the new capacitance, voltage, and energy when the gap doubles.

1. **Change the geometry.**

   $$C=\epsilon A/d\quad\Longrightarrow\quad C'=C/2$$

2. **Hold charge fixed.**

   $$V'=Q/C'=2V$$

3. **Use the fixed-charge energy form.**

   $$U'=Q^2/(2C')=2U$$

4. **Check the energy transfer.** The external agent’s positive separation work increases stored field energy.

**Result:** Capacitance halves, voltage doubles, and field energy doubles.

</section>

<section class="worked-application">

<h4 id="worked-m6h6">Worked application 6: Recovering a series component’s energy</h4>

Initially uncharged capacitors 3.0 μF and 6.0 μF are in series across 12 V, with a neutral isolated middle node. The calculation determines the energy stored in the 3.0 μF capacitor.

1. **Reduce the series pair.**

   $$C_{\mathrm{eq}}=\frac{3(6)}{3+6}=2\,\mu\mathrm F$$

2. **Find common charge.**

   $$Q=C_{\mathrm{eq}}V=2(12)=24\,\mu\mathrm C$$

3. **Recover the small capacitor’s voltage.**

   $$V_3=Q/C_3=24/3=8\,\mathrm V$$

4. **Calculate that component’s energy.** The 6 μF component has the remaining 4 V, so the recovered voltages add to 12 V.

   $$U_3=\tfrac12(3.0\times10^{-6})(8)^2$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$9.6\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm J$$

**Result:** $9.6\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm J$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m6h7">Worked application 7: Isolated dielectric insertion</h4>

A 2.0 μF capacitor is charged to 10 V and disconnected. An ideal dielectric of $\kappa=3$ fully fills the unchanged gap without leakage. The calculation determines the final stored energy.

1. **Find the charge before disconnection.**

   $$Q=(2.0\times10^{-6})(10)=20\,\mu\mathrm C$$

2. **Apply the dielectric change.**

   $$C'=3C=6.0\,\mu\mathrm F$$

3. **Hold charge fixed.**

   $$U'=\frac{Q^2}{2C'}=\frac{(20\times10^{-6})^2}{2(6.0\times10^{-6})}$$

4. **Check the comparison.** The initial energy is 100 μJ. Dividing by $\kappa=3$ gives the final field energy.

5. **State the result.**

   $$3.33333\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm J$$

**Result:** $3.33333\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm J$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m6h8">Worked application 8: Battery work during dielectric insertion</h4>

An ideal battery keeps 12 V across an initially 2.0 μF capacitor. A dielectric increases its capacitance to 8.0 μF during quasistatic insertion. The calculation determines the energy supplied by the battery, not just the capacitor-energy change.

1. **Find the capacitance increase.**

   $$\Delta C=(8.0-2.0)\,\mu\mathrm F=6.0\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$$

2. **Find charge delivered at fixed voltage.**

   $$\Delta Q=V\Delta C=12(6.0\times10^{-6})=72\,\mu\mathrm C$$

3. **Calculate source work.**

   $$W_{\mathrm{battery}}=V\Delta Q=12(72\times10^{-6})$$

4. **Check the larger energy account.** The rest can become mechanical work in the stated quasistatic idealization.

   $$\Delta U=\tfrac12\Delta CV^2=432\,\mu\mathrm J$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$8.64\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm J$$

**Result:** $8.64\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm J$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m6h9">Worked application 9: Geometry change after isolation</h4>

An ideal parallel-plate capacitor has area 0.030 m², gap 1.2 mm, and dielectric constant $\kappa=2$. It is charged to 100 V and isolated. The gap then doubles while the dielectric still completely fills it and κ remains fixed. The calculation determines the final stored energy. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{F\,m^{-1}}$.

1. **Convert the original gap.**

   $$d_0=1.2\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm m$$

2. **Find the initial capacitance.**

   $$C_0=\frac{\kappa\epsilon_0A}{d_0}=4.425\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm F$$

3. **Hold the isolated charge fixed as the gap doubles.**

   $$C_f=C_0/2,\qquad Q=C_0V_0$$

4. **Use the final capacitance with that fixed charge.** The final energy is twice the initial energy. External work separates the attracting plates.

   $$U_f=\frac{(C_0V_0)^2}{2(C_0/2)}=C_0V_0^2$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$4.425\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm J$$

**Result:** $4.425\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm J$

</section>

<h4 id="worked-m6h10">Worked application 10: Unequally charged capacitors share charge</h4>

Isolated capacitors 2.0 μF at 10 V and 3.0 μF at 4 V are connected in parallel with corresponding positive plates joined. A small resistance allows equilibrium. The calculation determines the final common voltage.

1. **Find charge on the joined positive node.**

   $$Q_{\mathrm{total}}=(2)(10)+(3)(4)=32\,\mu\mathrm C$$

2. **Find the final parallel capacitance.**

   $$C_f=2+3=5\,\mu\mathrm F$$

3. **Divide conserved charge by final capacitance.**

   $$V_f=32/5=6.4\,\mathrm V$$

4. **Check the energy difference.** The transient dissipates 21.6 μJ; conserving charge alone does not keep all initial energy in the final electric field.

   $$U_i=124\,\mu\mathrm J,\quad U_f=102.4\,\mu\mathrm J$$

5. **State the result.**

   $$6.4\,\mathrm V$$

**Result:** $6.4\,\mathrm V$

</section>

<h4 id="solution-m6h11">Worked application 11: A series capacitor feeds a parallel pair · the equivalent capacitance</h4>

An initially uncharged capacitor $C_1=3\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$ connects in series with a parallel pair $C_2=2.0\,\mu\mathrm F$ and $C_3=4\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$. An ideal 12 V source connects across the complete network. The internal connecting node starts neutral. Use the final steady electrostatic state. The calculation determines the equivalent capacitance.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Combine the parallel pair first. The series branch charges have equal magnitudes; the parallel capacitors share voltage.

2. **Reduce the parallel pair.**

   $$C_p=C_2+C_3=6\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$$

3. **Reduce the remaining series pair.**

   $$C_{\rm eq}=C_1C_p/(C_1+C_p)=2\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$$

4. **Recover branch charge and voltage.**

   $$Q_1=C_{\rm eq}V=2.4\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm C,\quad V_p=Q_1/C_p=4\,\mathrm V$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$C_{\rm eq}=\frac{3\times10^{-6}(6\times10^{-6})}{9\times10^{-6}}$$

6. **Check the physical result.** The total series capacitance is below either of its two branch capacitances.

7. **State the result.**

   $$2\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$$

**Result:** $2\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm F$

</section>

<h4 id="solution-m6h21">Worked application 21: A dielectric covers only part of the area · the final capacitance</h4>

A parallel-plate capacitor has area 0.03 m² and gap 0.001 m. A dielectric of relative permittivity 3 completely fills the gap over fraction 0.4 of the plate area. The remaining area contains vacuum. An ideal 100 V source remains connected. Ignore fringing. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{F\,m^{-1}}$. The calculation determines the final capacitance.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** The side-by-side regions have the same plate voltage, so they form parallel capacitances.

2. **Calculate the vacuum reference capacitance.**

   $$C_0=\varepsilon_0A/d=2.655\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm F$$

3. **Add the two area regions in parallel.**

   $$C=C_0[(1-f)+\kappa f]=4.779\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm F$$

4. **Apply the connected-source constraint.**

   $$V=\text{constant},\quad Q=CV,\quad U=\tfrac12CV^2$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$C=2.655\times10^{-10}[(1-0.4)+3(0.4)]$$

6. **Check the physical result.** Parallel area regions contribute capacitance additively.

7. **State the result.**

   $$4.779\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm F$$

**Result:** $4.779\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm F$

</section>

A parallel-plate capacitor has area 0.03 m² and gap 0.001 m. A dielectric of relative permittivity 3 completely fills the gap over fraction 0.4 of the plate area. The remaining area contains vacuum. An ideal 100 V source remains connected. Ignore fringing. Use $\varepsilon_0=8.85\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{F\,m^{-1}}$. The calculation determines the additional charge supplied by the source.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** The side-by-side regions have the same plate voltage, so they form parallel capacitances.

2. **Calculate the vacuum reference capacitance.**

   $$C_0=\varepsilon_0A/d=2.655\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm F$$

3. **Add the two area regions in parallel.**

   $$C=C_0[(1-f)+\kappa f]=4.779\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm F$$

4. **Apply the connected-source constraint.**

   $$V=\text{constant},\quad Q=CV,\quad U=\tfrac12CV^2$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$\Delta Q=(C-C_0)V=(4.779\times10^{-10}-2.655\times10^{-10})(100)$$

6. **Check the physical result.** The added source charge is the difference between the final and initial plate charges.

7. **State the result.**

   $$2.124\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm C$$

**Result:** $2.124\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm C$

<h4 id="solution-m6h31">Worked application 31: An isolated capacitor changes geometry · the final capacitance</h4>

A vacuum capacitor initially has capacitance $C_i=2\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm F$ and voltage 120 V. It is disconnected. Its plate area becomes 1.25 times the original, its gap becomes 2 times the original, and an ideal dielectric of relative permittivity 3 fills the new gap. Assume a quasistatic reversible change without leakage or dissipation. The calculation determines the final capacitance.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Isolation fixes charge, not voltage. Geometry and dielectric together determine the capacitance ratio.

2. **Calculate the new capacitance ratio.**

   $$\frac{C_f}{C_i}=\kappa\frac{A_f/A_i}{d_f/d_i}=3\frac{1.25}{2}$$

3. **Conserve the initial plate charge.**

   $$Q=C_iV_i=2.4\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm C$$

4. **Use charge-constrained voltage and energy.**

   $$V_f=Q/C_f,\quad U_f=Q^2/(2C_f),\quad W_{\rm ext}=U_f-U_i$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$C_f=C_i\kappa(1.25)/2=3.75\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm F$$

6. **Check the physical result.** All three geometry/material factors act on capacitance before charge conservation is applied.

7. **State the result.**

   $$3.75\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm F$$

**Result:** $3.75\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm F$

<h4 id="solution-m6h41">Worked application 41: Connect oppositely charged capacitors · the signed final common voltage</h4>

The other plates also join. A small resistance allows settling. Define the joined C1-positive node as the positive voltage reference. The calculation determines the signed final common voltage.

1. **Identify the model and the conditions.** Conserve signed charge on each joined node. Opposite initial polarities subtract; resistor heating accounts for lost field energy.

2. **Add signed charge on the reference node.**

   $$Q_{\rm node}=C_1V_1-C_2V_2=2.2\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm C$$

3. **Use the final parallel capacitance.**

   $$V_f=Q_{\rm node}/(C_1+C_2)=4.4\,\mathrm V$$

   $$U_i=\tfrac12C_1V_1^2+\tfrac12C_2V_2^2=1.66\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm J,\quad U_f=\tfrac12(C_1+C_2)V_f^2=4.84\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm J$$

5. **Solve the requested quantity.**

   $$V_f=\frac{C_1V_1-C_2V_2}{C_1+C_2}=4.4\,\mathrm V$$

6. **Check the physical result.** The positive result means C1’s initial reference-node charge dominates.

7. **State the result.**

   $$4.4\,\mathrm V$$

**Result:** $4.4\,\mathrm V$
