A capacitor stores energy in an electric field between conductors. Its plates carry +Q and −Q. In C=QVC = \frac{Q}{V}C=VQ​, Q is the magnitude on ONE plate, not the algebraic total charge of the pair. One farad is one coulomb per volt. Use μF=10−6 F\mathrm{\mu F} = 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{F}μF=10−6F, nF=10−9 F\mathrm{nF} = 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{F}nF=10−9F, and pF=10−12 F\mathrm{pF} = 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{F}pF=10−12F.

C=Q/V,Q=CV,V=Q/C.C=Q/V,\quad Q=CV,\quad V=Q/C.C=Q/V,Q=CV,V=Q/C.

For a linear ideal capacitor, geometry and dielectric determine C. A larger Q does not automatically mean a larger C: the applied voltage can increase in the same proportion. Capacitive keyboard keys exploit smaller separation increasing capacitance.

Derive parallel-plate capacitance

For large parallel plates with edge effects neglected, σ=QA\sigma = \frac{Q}{A}σ=AQ​ and E=σεE = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}E=εσ​. The approximately uniform gap field gives V=EdV = EdV=Ed. Substitute:

V=QdϵA⇒C=QV=ϵAd=κϵ0Ad.V=\frac{Qd}{\epsilon A}\Rightarrow C=\frac QV=\frac{\epsilon A}{d}=\frac{\kappa\epsilon_0 A}{d}.V=ϵAQd​⇒C=VQ​=dϵA​=dκϵ0​A​.

Here ε0≈8.85×10−12 Fm\varepsilon_{0} \approx 8.85 \times 10^{-12}\,\frac{\mathrm{F}}{\mathrm{m}}ε0​≈8.85×10−12mF​ and κ\kappaκ is a dimensionless dielectric constant. Rearrangements are A=Cd(κε0)A = \frac{Cd}{\left( \kappa \varepsilon_{0} \right)}A=(κε0​)Cd​, d=κε0ACd = \frac{\kappa \varepsilon_{0} A}{C}d=Cκε0​A​, κ=Cd(ε0A)\kappa = \frac{Cd}{\left( \varepsilon_{0} A \right)}κ=(ε0​A)Cd​. The parallel-plate model needs plate dimensions much larger than gap d. Do not use the same equation for arbitrary conductor shapes.

Dielectrics: connected versus disconnected battery

A dielectric polarizes and increases capacitance when it fills the gap: Cnew=κColdC_{\mathrm{new}} = \kappa C_{\mathrm{old}}Cnew​=κCold​. It can mechanically separate plates and permit greater voltage before breakdown, depending on the material and conditions.

Derive these changes. Start with Q=CVQ = CVQ=CV. Fix V in the connected case or Q in the isolated case. Then use U=CV22U = \frac{CV^{2}}{2}U=2CV2​ or Q2(2 C)\frac{Q^{2}}{\left( 2\,\mathrm{C} \right)}(2C)Q2​. The connected capacitor can gain stored energy because the battery exchanges energy and charge. Networks: derive series and parallel rules

Their voltage drops add: Vtot=Q∑(1Ci)V_{\mathrm{tot}} = Q\sum \left( \frac{1}{C_{i}} \right)Vtot​=Q∑(Ci​1​), so 1Ceq=∑i1Ci\frac1{C_{\mathrm{eq}}}=\sum_i\frac1{C_i}Ceq​1​=∑i​Ci​1​.
