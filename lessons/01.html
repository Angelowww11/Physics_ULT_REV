A fluid can flow. Liquids and gases are fluids. Fluid statics studies equilibrium; fluid dynamics studies motion. In the molecular model, gas particles are widely separated and compressible. A liquid has a definite volume but takes its container's shape. The hydrostatic formulas below require a stationary fluid, not an accelerating flow.

Density, relative density, and unit conversions

Density measures mass per volume. Relative density compares two densities and has no unit. Use the fluid density in buoyancy calculations, not the object's density.

ρ=mV,m=ρV,V=mρ,w=mg=ρVg,SG=ρρwater.\rho=\frac mV,\quad m=\rho V,\quad V=\frac m\rho,\quad w=mg=\rho Vg,\quad SG=\frac{\rho}{\rho_{water}}.ρ=Vm​,m=ρV,V=ρm​,w=mg=ρVg,SG=ρwater​ρ​.

A hollow object's average density includes its empty interior. Water is about 1000 kgm31000\,\frac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{m}^{3}}1000m3kg​ in the classroom model. Convert 1gcm3\frac{1 g}{\mathrm{cm}^{3}}cm31g​ to 1000 kgm31000\,\frac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{m}^{3}}1000m3kg​. Also use 1L=10−3 m31 L = 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}^{3}1L=10−3m3, 1 mL=1 cm31\,\mathrm{mL} = 1\,\mathrm{cm}^{3}1mL=1cm3, and 1 cm3=10−6 m31\,\mathrm{cm}^{3} = 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}^{3}1cm3=10−6m3. Pressure and the hydrostatic derivation

Pressure is normal force per area: P=F⊥AP = \frac{F_{\perp}}{A}P=AF⊥​​. One pascal equals one Nm2\frac{N}{m^{2}}m2N​. Rearrange to F⊥=PAF_{\perp} = PAF⊥​=PA or A=F⊥PA = \frac{F_{\perp}}{P}A=PF⊥​​. Pressure is a scalar; the force on each wall points perpendicular to that wall. A static fluid cannot sustain a shear stress.

Derivation. Isolate a vertical fluid column of area A and depth h. The downward forces are P0AP_{0} AP0​A and its weight ρAhg\rho AhgρAhg. The upward force is PbottomA. Equilibrium gives:

PbA−P0A−ρAhg=0⇒Pb=P0+ρgh.P_bA-P_0A-\rho Ahg=0\Rightarrow P_b=P_0+\rho gh.Pb​A−P0​A−ρAhg=0⇒Pb​=P0​+ρgh.

The area cancels. This explains why pressure at a given depth does not depend on container shape. Equal pressure at equal depth requires the same connected, stationary fluid under the same surface conditions.

ΔP=ρgh,h=ΔPρg,ρ=ΔPgh,F=(P0+ρgh)A.\Delta P=\rho gh,\quad h=\frac{\Delta P}{\rho g},\quad \rho=\frac{\Delta P}{gh},\quad F=(P_0+\rho gh)A.ΔP=ρgh,h=ρgΔP​,ρ=ghΔP​,F=(P0​+ρgh)A.

Pressure differences across a wall use the pressure on BOTH sides. Do not multiply an absolute pressure by area when the question asks the net force against outside air. For nonconstant density, the general vertical relation is dPdz=−ρ(z)g\frac{dP}{dz} = - \rho \left( z \right) gdzdP​=−ρ(z)g; integrate instead of assuming ρgh\rho ghρgh.

Pascal's principle and hydraulic displacement

An applied pressure change travels through a confined fluid. At equal piston elevations and neglecting friction, ΔP1=ΔP2\Delta P_{1} = \Delta P_{2}ΔP1​=ΔP2​:

F1A1=F2A2,F2=F1A2A1=F1(r2r1)2.\frac{F_1}{A_1}=\frac{F_2}{A_2},\quad F_2=F_1\frac{A_2}{A_1}=F_1\left(\frac{r_2}{r_1}\right)^2.A1​F1​​=A2​F2​​,F2​=F1​A1​A2​​=F1​(r1​r2​​)2.

Derive the distance relation. Conservation of incompressible fluid volume gives A1d1=A2d2A_{1} d_{1} = A_{2} d_{2}A1​d1​=A2​d2​. Thus d2d1=A1A2\frac{d_{2}}{d_{1}} = \frac{A_{1}}{A_{2}}d1​d2​​=A2​A1​​. Multiplying by the force ratio gives F1d1=F2d2F_{1} d_{1} = F_{2} d_{2}F1​d1​=F2​d2​: force multiplication is not energy creation. For unequal piston heights, include the hydrostatic pressure difference before applying the force balance.

Archimedes' principle: derive it before choosing a volume

Pressure at a submerged block's bottom exceeds the pressure at its top. For constant area A, the net upward fluid force is (Pbottom−Ptop)A=ρfg(hbottom−htop)A\left( P_{\mathrm{bottom}} - P_{\mathrm{top}} \right) A = \rho_{f} g \left( h_{\mathrm{bottom}} - h_{\mathrm{top}} \right) A(Pbottom​−Ptop​)A=ρf​g(hbottom​−htop​)A. Since AΔh=VdisplacedA\Delta h = V_{\mathrm{displaced}}AΔh=Vdisplaced​:

FB=ρfgVdisp.F_B=\rho_f gV_{disp}.FB​=ρf​gVdisp​.

This relation uses the volume of displaced fluid. For a fully submerged impermeable body, it equals the object's external volume. For partial immersion, it does not equal the whole object volume. A fluid's pressure gradient produces buoyancy even without a free surface immediately beside the object.

For a hanging object in equilibrium, T+FB−mg=0T + F_{B} - mg = 0T+FB​−mg=0, so T=mg−FBT = mg - F_{B}T=mg−FB​. Apparent weight equals that tension. If a light object is tied DOWN, FB−mg−T=0F_{B} - mg - T = 0FB​−mg−T=0, so T=FB−mgT = F_{B} - mgT=FB​−mg. Choose the sign from the diagram.

Floating, sinking, and identifying unknown density

For free floating equilibrium, FB=mgF_{B} = mgFB​=mg. With m=ρobjectVtotalm = \rho _{\mathrm{object}}V_{\mathrm{total}}m=ρobject​Vtotal​:

VsubVtotal=ρobjectρf,ρf=ρobjectVtotalVsub.\frac{V_{sub}}{V_{total}}=\frac{\rho_{object}}{\rho_f},\quad \rho_f=\rho_{object}\frac{V_{total}}{V_{sub}}.Vtotal​Vsub​​=ρf​ρobject​​,ρf​=ρobject​Vsub​Vtotal​​.

This fraction must lie between zero and one. If the average object density exceeds the fluid density, a freely floating equilibrium with partial immersion is impossible. A sinking object still has an upward buoyant force; that force is simply insufficient to balance its weight. Neutral buoyancy means equal average densities, not zero buoyancy.

Worked example — primary p. 34. One-third of a person's volume is above the surface, so two-thirds is submerged. With average density 970 kgm3970\,\frac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{m}^{3}}970m3kg​, fluid density: 970(23)=1455 kgm3\frac{970}{\left( \frac{2}{3} \right)} = 1455\,\frac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{m}^{3}}(32​)970​=1455m3kg​. The printed 1460 is a rounded value. The assumed 3 m33\,\mathrm{m}^{3}3m3 is only a convenient volume ratio, not a realistic measured body volume.

Take note. Primary p. 12 labels density with Pa; its unit must be kgm3\frac{\mathrm{kg}}{m^{3}}m3kg​. Floating formulas require equilibrium without an extra vertical support force. Do not use them for a block held underwater by a string.

Derivation extension — weighing an unknown solid. Measure the weight Wair in air, then the apparent weight Wsub while fully submerged. Neglect air buoyancy and string mass. The difference is the fluid buoyancy: Wair−Wsub=ρfgVW_{\mathrm{air}} - W_{\mathrm{sub}} = \rho_{f} gVWair​−Wsub​=ρf​gV. Meanwhile Wair=ρobjectgVW_{\mathrm{air}} = \rho _{\mathrm{object}} gVWair​=ρobject​gV. Divide the equations to remove both g and V:

ρobject=ρfWairWair−Wsub.\rho_{object}=\rho_f\frac{W_{air}}{W_{air}-W_{sub}}.ρobject​=ρf​Wair​−Wsub​Wair​​.

For Wair=12.0 NW_{\mathrm{air}} = 12.0\,\mathrm{N}Wair​=12.0N and Wsub=8.0 NW_{\mathrm{sub}} = 8.0\,\mathrm{N}Wsub​=8.0N in water, density: 1000(12)(12−8)=3000 kgm3\frac{1000 \left( 12 \right)}{\left( 12 - 8 \right)} = 3000\,\frac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{m}^{3}}(12−8)1000(12)​=3000m3kg​. A smaller apparent weight means a larger buoyant force. This formula does not apply unchanged to an object resting on the bottom.

Why a fluid has pressure — companion pp. 3–4. Molecular motion and interactions transfer momentum to container walls. Gravity creates a vertical pressure gradient, but it is not the only cause of pressure. Gas molecules still collide with walls in weightlessness. Over a small laboratory height, a gas's gravitational pressure change can be small; atmospheric pressure changes substantially over larger heights. Internal and external pressure partly balance around the body, so atmospheric pressure does not act as an unopposed inward force everywhere.
