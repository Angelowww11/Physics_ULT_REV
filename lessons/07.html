Current is signed charge flow per time: I=dQdtI = \frac{dQ}{dt}I=dtdQ​, or QΔt\frac{Q}{\Delta t}ΔtQ​ for a constant average flow. One ampere is one Cs\frac{C}{s}sC​. Temperature, ohmic behavior, and resistor identification

Near a reference temperature, R=R0[1+α(T−T0)]R = R_{0} \left[ 1 + \alpha \left( T - T_{0} \right) \right]R=R0​[1+α(T−T0​)], and similarly for resistivity. Rearrange α=(RR0−1)(T−T0)\alpha = \frac{\left( \frac{R}{R_{0}} - 1 \right)}{\left( T - T_{0} \right)}α=(T−T0​)(R0​R​−1)​. This linear approximation can fail over a large temperature range. Do not confuse the resistance-temperature coefficient with thermal-expansion α\alphaα. R0R_{0}R0​ is resistance when used in the R equation, despite the source's occasional “resistivity” label.

Ohm's law means a linear V–I relation under stable material conditions. A lamp heating up need not have a constant resistance. Carbon/semiconductor temperature behavior can differ from metals.

The source includes resistor color bands. For a four-band resistor, the first two colors give digits, the third gives a power-of-ten multiplier, and the fourth gives tolerance. Digit sequence: black 0, brown 1, red 2, orange 3, yellow 4, green 5, blue 6, violet 7, gray 8, white 9. Gold/silver commonly represent multipliers 10−110−2\frac{10^{-1}}{10^{-2}}10−210−1​ or tolerances ±5%/±10%. Read the band roles and orientation, not only the colors.

Kirchhoff rules: derive the equations and keep the signs

KCL follows conservation of charge at a steady node: ∑Iin=∑Iout\sum I_{\mathrm{in}} = \sum I_{\mathrm{out}}∑Iin​=∑Iout​. KVL follows energy balance around a lumped DC loop: ∑ΔV=0\sum \Delta V = 0∑ΔV=0. A resistor traversed with the chosen current gives −IR; traversed against it gives +IR. Across an ideal battery, negative-to-positive gives +E+ \mathcal{E}+E and the reverse gives −E- \mathcal{E}−E.

Choose arbitrary current directions, write independent equations, and solve them simultaneously. A negative answer reverses the assumed direction; it is not a negative physical resistance. Do not change an assumed arrow midway through the algebra.
