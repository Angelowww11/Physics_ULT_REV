Like magnetic poles repel and unlike poles attract. Breaking a bar magnet makes smaller dipoles, not isolated ordinary north and south poles. Outside a bar magnet, conventional field lines go north to south; they continue through the magnet to form closed loops. A compass's north-seeking end gives the local field direction. Earth's geographic and magnetic pole descriptions are not identical.

Oersted's current-carrying wire deflects a compass, showing that current creates a magnetic field. Do not confuse this source-field right-hand grip rule with the force cross-product rule.

Force on a moving charge and on a wire

FB=qv×B,FB=∣q∣vBsin⁡θ.\mathbf F_B=q\mathbf v\times\mathbf B,\quad F_B=|q|vB\sin\theta.FB​=qv×B,FB​=∣q∣vBsinθ.

Torque on a current loop

Opposite sides of a loop in uniform B have equal and opposite forces. Net force can vanish while torque remains. The lever-arm geometry yields:

μ=NIA,τ=μ×B,τ=NIABsin⁡θ,U=−μ⋅B.\begin{gathered}\boldsymbol\mu=NI\mathbf A,\\\boldsymbol\tau=\boldsymbol\mu\times\mathbf B,\\\tau=NIAB\sin\theta,\\U=-\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf B.\end{gathered}μ=NIA,τ=μ×B,τ=NIABsinθ,U=−μ⋅B.​

Here θ\thetaθ is between the AREA NORMAL and B, not between the loop's plane and B. For angle ϕ\phiϕ to the plane, sin⁡θ=cos⁡ϕ\sin \theta = \cos \phisinθ=cosϕ. Maximum torque occurs when the normal is perpendicular to B. Zero torque occurs when the normal is parallel or antiparallel; the parallel orientation has the lower potential energy.

A galvanometer turns a current-carrying coil against a restoring effect. Analog meters build on that principle. A motor converts electrical energy to mechanical energy; a loudspeaker moves a coil and cone with a varying force.

Magnetism, field notation, and the three right-hand rules

Source context — primary pp. 4–11. The historical material describes magnetite and the name Magnesia. A magnetized needle aligns with the local field. Either end of a magnet can attract an initially unmagnetized iron nail because the nail becomes magnetized. Splitting a magnet produces smaller dipoles. It does not separate an ordinary bar magnet into isolated north and south poles.

Earth and a compass. The geographic poles describe the rotation axis. Magnetic-pole locations and the local field direction differ from that axis. Near geographic north, Earth's field can attract a compass's north-seeking end. Keep the pole-naming convention separate from the field direction used in a force calculation.

Wire-force law from the motion of charge carriers

Source laws: Primary pp. 12–17. Let the carrier number density be n, wire cross section A, and segment length L. Use a uniform external field.

  1. Count the carriers inside the segment: Ncarriers=nALN_{\mathrm{carriers}} = nALNcarriers​=nAL.
  2. Add their magnetic forces: Ftotal=nALq(vd×B)F_{\mathrm{total}} = nALq \left( v_{d} \times B \right)Ftotal​=nALq(vd​×B).
  3. Use the signed carrier-current relation nqvd=Jnqv_{d} = Jnqvd​=J, with conventional-current direction encoded in J.
  4. Replace JA by the current and direct the length vector along that current.
F=IL×B,F=ILBsin⁡θ.\mathbf F=I\mathbf L\times\mathbf B, \qquad F=ILB\sin\theta.F=IL×B,F=ILBsinθ.

This derivation removes the microscopic carrier density and drift speed. L means the length inside the field. For a curved path in a nonuniform field, integrate Idl×BI dl \times BIdl×B instead of using one segment's product.

Sources: Primary pp. 37–42. Use a rectangular Amperian path with an interior segment of length x parallel to the solenoid axis.

  1. The transverse path segments have B perpendicular to dl, so their dot-product contributions vanish.
